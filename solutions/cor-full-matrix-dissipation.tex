\section*{Solution candidate: full matrix Riccati dissipation}
\label{sec:sol-full-matrix-dissipation}

\paragraph{Scope and notation.}
Let $\mu$ be an isotropic log-concave probability measure on $\R^n$, let $E$ be a fixed
measurable set with $0<\mu(E)<1$, and run Eldan stochastic localization.  We use the standing
two-color notation
\[
 p_t=\mu_t(E),\qquad q_t=1-p_t,\qquad s_t=p_tq_t,
\]
\[
 B_t=s_t\delta_t\delta_t^T,
 \qquad
 R_t=A_t-B_t=p_t\Sigma_t^E+q_t\Sigma_t^{E^c}\succeq0,
 \qquad
 G_t=\Sigma_t^E-\Sigma_t^{E^c}.
\]
The finite-time localization density is strictly positive relative to $\mu$, so the two
conditional laws, and hence these matrices, are defined at every finite time.  All matrix
integrals below are understood entrywise; the proof shows their absolute integrability rather
than assuming it.

\begin{theorem}[refined form of Corollary~\ref{cor:full-matrix-dissipation}]
\label{thm:sol-full-matrix-dissipation}
For every finite $T\geq0$,
\begin{equation}
 \E R_T+
 \E\int_0^T\bigl(R_t^2+s_tG_t^2\bigr)\dd t
 \preceq R_0.
 \label{eq:sol-full-matrix-finite}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \E\int_0^\infty\bigl(R_t^2+s_tG_t^2\bigr)\dd t
 \preceq R_0\preceq I_n.
 \label{eq:sol-full-matrix-infinite}
\end{equation}
No balance or stopping-window hypothesis is required.
\end{theorem}

We first isolate the only stochastic expectation argument that is needed.

\begin{lemma}[Positive matrix drift survives localization]
\label{lem:sol-positive-matrix-drift}
Let $(X_t)_{t\geq0}$ be a continuous adapted process with values in the positive semidefinite
symmetric matrices, with deterministic initial matrix $X_0$.  Suppose that, entrywise,
\begin{equation}
 X_t=X_0+N_t-\int_0^tQ_u\dd u,
 \label{eq:sol-abstract-matrix-decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $N_0=0$ is a matrix local martingale and $Q_t\succeq0$ is progressively measurable and
locally integrable.  Then the matrices in the following display are integrable and, for every
finite $T$,
\begin{equation}
 \E X_T+\E\int_0^TQ_t\dd t\preceq X_0.
 \label{eq:sol-abstract-finite}
\end{equation}
Moreover the improper integral $\int_0^\infty Q_t\dd t$ exists entrywise and absolutely almost
surely, is integrable, and
\begin{equation}
 \E\int_0^\infty Q_t\dd t\preceq X_0.
 \label{eq:sol-abstract-infinite}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a deterministic vector $\theta\in\R^n$ and set
\[
 x_t^\theta=\theta^TX_t\theta,
 \qquad
 M_t^\theta=\theta^TN_t\theta,
 \qquad
 a_t^\theta=\int_0^t\theta^TQ_u\theta\dd u.
\]
Thus $x^\theta,a^\theta\geq0$, $a^\theta$ is increasing, and
\begin{equation}
 x_t^\theta+a_t^\theta=x_0^\theta+M_t^\theta.
 \label{eq:sol-scalarized-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Choose increasing localizing times $\sigma_k\uparrow\infty$ almost surely such that
$(M^\theta)^{\sigma_k}$ is a true martingale.  At the bounded time $T\wedge\sigma_k$,
\eqref{eq:sol-scalarized-decomposition} and optional sampling give
\begin{equation}
 \E\bigl[x_{T\wedge\sigma_k}^\theta+a_{T\wedge\sigma_k}^\theta\bigr]
 =x_0^\theta.
 \label{eq:sol-stopped-expectation}
\end{equation}
There is no integrability assumption hidden here: the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:sol-scalarized-decomposition} is integrable after stopping, while its left-hand side
is nonnegative, so both terms on that side are integrable.

Continuity of $X$ and local integrability of $Q$ imply convergence of the stopped left-hand
side to $x_T^\theta+a_T^\theta$.  Fatou's lemma, applied to that nonnegative sum, now yields
\begin{equation}
 \E x_T^\theta+\E a_T^\theta\leq x_0^\theta.
 \label{eq:sol-directional-finite}
\end{equation}
In particular this estimate does not require uniform integrability of the unstopped local
martingale or of the terminal matrix process.

For completeness, the scalar quadratic-form estimates really do reconstruct ordinary
integrable matrices.  Apply \eqref{eq:sol-directional-finite} to the coordinate vectors
$e_i$.  It gives integrability of the diagonal entries of $X_T$ and
$H_T:=\int_0^TQ_t\dd t$.  Positivity gives, pathwise,
\[
 \abs{(X_T)_{ij}}\leq\frac{(X_T)_{ii}+(X_T)_{jj}}2,
 \qquad
 \int_0^T\abs{(Q_t)_{ij}}\dd t
 \leq\frac{(H_T)_{ii}+(H_T)_{jj}}2.
\]
Hence all entries are absolutely integrable.  Polarization, or simply testing
\eqref{eq:sol-directional-finite} for every deterministic $\theta$, gives
\[
 \theta^T\left(\E X_T+\E H_T\right)\theta
 \leq\theta^TX_0\theta
 \qquad(\theta\in\R^n),
\]
which is exactly the Loewner inequality \eqref{eq:sol-abstract-finite}.

Finally $a_T^\theta\uparrow a_\infty^\theta$ as $T\uparrow\infty$.  Monotone convergence in
\eqref{eq:sol-directional-finite} gives
\[
 \E a_\infty^\theta\leq x_0^\theta.
\]
For $\theta=e_i$, this says that every diagonal integral is finite almost surely and in
$L^1$.  The positive-semidefinite entry bound just used, now on $[0,\infty)$, makes every
off-diagonal integral absolutely convergent and integrable.  Polarization reconstructs the
matrix expectation, and the preceding inequality for all $\theta$ proves
\eqref{eq:sol-abstract-infinite}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sol-full-matrix-dissipation}]
The certified matrix Riccati identity, Lemma~\ref{lem:matrix-riccati}, is
\begin{equation}
 \dd R_t=\dd\widetilde N_t-\bigl(R_t^2+s_tG_t^2\bigr)\dd t,
 \label{eq:sol-certified-matrix-riccati}
\end{equation}
for a symmetric matrix local martingale $\widetilde N$.  Covariance decomposition gives
$R_t\succeq0$.  Both $R_t$ and $G_t$ are symmetric and $s_t\geq0$, so
\[
 Q_t:=R_t^2+s_tG_t^2\succeq0.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:sol-positive-matrix-drift}, with $X=R$ and this $Q$, proves
\eqref{eq:sol-full-matrix-finite} and the first inequality in
\eqref{eq:sol-full-matrix-infinite}.  At time zero,
$R_0=A_0-B_0\preceq A_0=I_n$ by isotropy, proving the second one.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Relation to the per-direction estimate and the trace gate.}
Testing \eqref{eq:sol-full-matrix-infinite} against any deterministic $\theta$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \E\int_0^\infty
 \left(\abs{R_t\theta}^2+s_t\abs{G_t\theta}^2\right)\dd t
 \leq\theta^TR_0\theta.
 \label{eq:sol-full-matrix-directional}
\end{equation}
Restricting the integral to $[0,T]$ recovers the first display of
Corollary~\ref{cor:per-direction}; discarding the nonnegative $R_t^2$ term recovers its
source-only Loewner display.  Thus the new conclusion is the infinite-horizon matrix form of
the full directional dissipation and retains strictly more information than the source-only
bound.

The extra information is non-vacuous.  For the standard Gaussian measure and the halfspace
$E=\{x_1\leq0\}$, every localization posterior remains a product across the coordinate axes
and has covariance $(1+t)^{-1}I_n$.  For each spectator direction $e_j$, $j\geq2$, conditioning
on $E$ does not change that coordinate, and hence
\[
 G_te_j=0,
 \qquad
 R_te_j=(1+t)^{-1}e_j,
 \qquad
 \int_0^\infty\abs{R_te_j}^2\dd t=1.
\]
The source-only estimate sees zero in those directions, whereas the full dissipation estimate
uses their entire unit budgets.

Taking a trace in \eqref{eq:sol-full-matrix-infinite} gives only
\begin{equation}
 \E\int_0^\infty
 \left(\norm{R_t}_{\HS}^2+s_t\norm{G_t}_{\HS}^2\right)\dd t
 \leq\Tr R_0\leq n.
 \label{eq:sol-full-matrix-trace}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the Gaussian halfspace example already contributes $n-1$ from its spectator
$R_t^2$ directions.  The result therefore supplies a genuine matrix coercivity budget but no
dimension-free operator-to-trace upgrade.

\paragraph{Hypotheses and fences.}
The only mathematical input beyond the standing two-color setup is the already certified
matrix Riccati identity.  Isotropy is used solely for $R_0\preceq I_n$; the estimate with right
side $R_0$ holds for any initial law for which that identity and the two-color covariances are
defined.  Nontriviality of the cut is exactly what makes the conditional covariances meaningful.
No balance, smooth-boundary, compact-support, stopping-time, fourth-moment, or endpoint uniform-
integrability hypothesis is used.  The ledger node has no \texttt{bounded\_by} edge.  The
dimension-dependent trace consequence \eqref{eq:sol-full-matrix-trace} explicitly respects the
operator-to-trace obstruction emphasized immediately after the manuscript corollary.

\section*{Solution: symmetrizing a variational law}

\begin{lemma}
Let a finite group $G$ act isometrically on $\Theta$, let $\pi$ be $G$-invariant, and put
$\bar q=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g_\#q$ for $q\ll\pi$ in $\mathcal P_2(\Theta)$. Then every
integrable invariant observable $f$ satisfies $\E_{\bar q}f=\E_qf$, and
\[
 \KL(\bar q\|\pi)\le\KL(q\|\pi),\qquad
 W_2^2(\bar q,\pi)\le W_2^2(q,\pi).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For invariant $f$, each pushforward obeys $\E_{g_\#q}f=\E_q(f\circ g)=\E_qf$, and averaging
proves the first assertion. Convexity of relative entropy in its first argument and invariance of
$\pi$ give
\[
 \KL(\bar q\|\pi)
 \le |G|^{-1}\sum_g\KL(g_\#q\|\pi)=\KL(q\|\pi).
\]
For each $g$, push an optimal (or arbitrarily near-optimal) coupling of $q$ and $\pi$ through
$(g,g)$. It couples $g_\#q$ and $g_\#\pi=\pi$ with unchanged cost. Averaging these couplings
has marginals $\bar q$ and $\pi$ and the same average cost, proving the Wasserstein inequality.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Obstruction respected.}
This statement preserves invariant observables only. It neither removes physical multimodality
nor orders the ratio of the two quantities that decrease, and it applies within a variational
optimization only if the family admits the group average.

\section*{Solution: the spectator obstruction to a superlinear excess remainder}
\label{sec:sol-spectator-excess-rate-obstruction}

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Let $\lambda$ be the law of $Y-1$, where $Y$ has the rate-one exponential law.  Thus
$\lambda$ is centered, log-concave, and has variance one, with density
\[
  w_1(x)=e^{-(x+1)}\one_{\{x\ge-1\}}.
\]
All perimeters use the lower outer Minkowski convention of the manuscript.  For the regular
sets selected below, this agrees with relative weighted BV perimeter inside the convex support.
For a localized law $\mu_t$, a fixed cut $E$, and $p_t=\mu_t(E)$, write
\[
  P_t(E)=\mu_t^+(E),\qquad
  e_t(E)=P_t(E)-I_{\mu_t}(p_t),\qquad
  \tau_\eta=\inf\{t:|p_t-\tfrac12|>\eta\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Spectator obstruction to a superlinear excess remainder;
Proposition~\ref{prop:spectator-excess-rate-obstruction}]
\label{thm:sol-spectator-excess-rate-obstruction}
For every $C,T_0,\gamma>0$, every $\eta\in(0,1/2)$, and every $\delta>0$, there are an
integer $d$, the isotropic log-concave product $\mu=\lambda^{\otimes d}$, a finite-perimeter
cylinder $E\subset\R^d$ with $\mu(E)=1/2$ and
\[
  e_0(E)\le\delta,
  \qquad
  \frac{e_0(E)}{I_\mu(1/2)}\le\delta,
\]
and a time $0<T\le T_0$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}e_t(E)\dd t
  >C\bigl(Te_0(E)+T^{1+\gamma}\bigr).
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-violation}
\end{equation}
The construction can additionally be made with $e_0(E)\le1$.  Thus even the unweighted
excess has no uniform remainder of the displayed source-vanishing superlinear form.  In
particular, within the usual normalized class of replacement weights $W_t\ge1$, changing only
the global covariance weight cannot repair that rate.  This does not refute KLS: every witness
is a product measure and has a dimension-free KLS constant by Proposition~\ref{prop:products}.
\end{theorem}

We first record the regular selection and change-of-density statement needed for the exact
noncompact one-sided-exponential product.

\begin{lemma}[Regular competitors for the exponential product]
\label{lem:sol-spectator-rate-regularization}
Let $\nu=\lambda^{\otimes m}$ and let $p\in(0,1)$.  For every $\rho>0$ there is a relative
weighted-BV set $S$, regular in the interior of $K_m=[-1,\infty)^m$, such that
\[
  \nu(S)=p,
  \qquad
  \nu^+(S)\le I_\nu(p)+\rho.
\]
For this $S$, lower outer Minkowski perimeter equals relative weighted perimeter.  Whenever
$t\ge0$ and
\[
  0<Z(u,t):=\int\exp(u\cdot x-t|x|^2/2)\dd\nu(x)<\infty,
\]
define
\[
  \dd\nu_{u,t}=F_{u,t}\dd\nu,
  \qquad
  F_{u,t}(x)=\frac{\exp(u\cdot x-t|x|^2/2)}{Z(u,t)}.
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
  \nu_{u,t}^+(S)=\int F_{u,t}\dd\sigma_S,
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-density-change}
\end{equation}
where
\[
  \dd\sigma_S=e^{-\sum_{j=1}^m(x_j+1)}
  \dd\mathcal H^{m-1}|_{\partial^*S\cap\operatorname{int}K_m}
\]
is the relative weighted perimeter measure.  There is no surface term on $\partial K_m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On compact subsets of $\operatorname{int}K_m$, the density of $\nu$ is smooth and strictly
positive.  Choose a mass-$p$ competitor $S^{(0)}$ whose lower outer Minkowski perimeter is
within $\rho/4$ of $I_\nu(p)$.  Finite lower outer Minkowski perimeter gives finite relative BV
perimeter, no larger than that Minkowski content.  Truncate in $K_m\cap B_R$, mollify the
indicator in interior and boundary charts, use coarea, and let $R\uparrow\infty$.  The usual
diagonal choice gives regular relative sets $S_k$ for which
\[
  \nu(S_k)\longrightarrow p,
  \qquad
  \operatorname{Per}_\nu(S_k)\longrightarrow
  \operatorname{Per}_\nu(S^{(0)}).
\]
If necessary, a compactly supported smooth flow through a regular boundary patch in
$\operatorname{int}K_m$ corrects the remaining mass error exactly.  Its mass derivative is the
strictly positive weighted area of the patch, while its perimeter changes continuously, so the
correction costs $o(1)$ perimeter.  Choose a sufficiently advanced exact-mass corrected
approximant and rename it $S$.  It has the asserted perimeter bound.

For a regular relative set, the one-sided tube formula inside $K_m$ identifies outer Minkowski
content with
\[
  \int_{\partial^*S\cap\operatorname{int}K_m}
       e^{-\sum_j(x_j+1)}\dd\mathcal H^{m-1}.
\]
Topological-boundary pieces on $\partial K_m$ are support boundary, not interfaces in the
support, and contribute neither to this relative formula nor to the ambient Minkowski
derivative, since $\nu$ vanishes outside $K_m$.  Multiplication by the positive continuous
factor $F_{u,t}$ leaves the relative reduced boundary unchanged and multiplies its weighted
surface measure by $F_{u,t}$.  The same tube formula proves
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-density-change}.  Thus no compact approximation of the probability
law is used later.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:sol-spectator-excess-rate-obstruction}]
\emph{Step 1: select one base cylinder before selecting time or spectator dimension.}
For $m\ge1$, set
\[
  a_m:=I_{\lambda^{\otimes m}}(1/2).
\]
If $S\subset\R^m$, then
\[
  \dist\bigl((x,z),S\times\R\bigr)=\dist(x,S),
  \qquad
  (S\times\R)_r=S_r\times\R.
\]
Consequently cylinder extension preserves both mass and lower outer Minkowski perimeter, and
$a_{m+1}\le a_m$.

Proposition~\ref{prop:products} gives a universal $h_*>0$ with
$h_{\lambda^{\otimes m}}\ge h_*$ for every $m$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:half} therefore gives
\begin{equation}
  a_m=\frac12h_{\lambda^{\otimes m}}\ge a_*:=\frac{h_*}{2}>0.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-profile-floor}
\end{equation}
Thus $a_m\downarrow a_\infty$ for some $a_\infty\ge a_*>0$.

Put
\[
  h_{\rm sp}:=1-e^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \kappa_{\rm sp}:=\frac{h_{\rm sp}}{128}>0.
\]
Choose $\varepsilon>0$ strictly smaller than
\begin{equation}
  \min\left\{\delta,\delta a_*,1,
  \frac{\kappa_{\rm sp}a_*}{4C}\right\}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-epsilon}
\end{equation}
Choose $M$ so that $a_M-a_\infty<\varepsilon/2$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:sol-spectator-rate-regularization} at $p=1/2$ with $\rho=\varepsilon/2$ to
obtain a regular set $E_0\subset\R^M$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \lambda^{\otimes M}(E_0)=\frac12,
  \qquad
  P_0:=\lambda^{\otimes M,+}(E_0)\le a_M+\frac\varepsilon2.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-base}
\end{equation}
By the definition of $a_M$, also $P_0\ge a_M\ge a_*>0$.

For every integer $N\ge1$, put
\[
  \mu^{M,N}=\lambda^{\otimes M}\otimes\lambda^{\otimes N},
  \qquad
  E^{M,N}=E_0\times\R^N.
\]
The cylinder has mass $1/2$ and perimeter $P_0$, and hence
\begin{align}
  0\le e_0(E^{M,N})
  &=P_0-a_{M+N}<\varepsilon,
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-additive}\\
  \frac{e_0(E^{M,N})}{I_{\mu^{M,N}}(1/2)}
  &<\frac{\varepsilon}{a_*}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-relative}
\end{align}
These estimates hold uniformly in every spectator dimension that will be chosen later.  By
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-epsilon}, they imply both requested $\delta$ bounds and
$e_0(E^{M,N})\le1$.

\emph{Step 2: construct an independent positive-probability base-stability event.}
Use the planted Gaussian observation realization of stochastic localization.  Write
\[
  X=(X^0,X^1,\ldots,X^N),
  \qquad
  c_t=tX+B_t,
\]
where $X^0\sim\lambda^{\otimes M}$, $X^i\sim\lambda$ for $i\ge1$, and all latent blocks and
Brownian coordinate blocks are independent.  Given the observation up to time $t$, its
endpoint is sufficient and the posterior is
\[
  \dd\mu_t(x)=Z_t^{-1}
  \exp\bigl(c_t\cdot x-t|x|^2/2\bigr)\dd\mu^{M,N}(x).
\]
The prior and likelihood factorize, so pathwise
\begin{equation}
  \mu_t=\nu_t^0\otimes\bigotimes_{i=1}^N\lambda_{i,t},
  \qquad
  A_t=\operatorname{diag}(A_t^0,v_{1,t},\ldots,v_{N,t}),
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-factorization}
\end{equation}
where $v_{i,t}=\Var(\lambda_{i,t})$.  The base posterior process is independent of the
spectator posterior processes.  The cylinder and distance identities give
\begin{equation}
  p_t=\nu_t^0(E_0),
  \qquad
  P_t(E^{M,N})=(\nu_t^0)^+(E_0),
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-localized-cylinder}
\end{equation}
so $p_t$, the tracked perimeter, and $\tau_\eta$ are all base-block measurable.

For deterministic $(u,t)$ in the base block, write
\[
  F_{u,t}(x)=
  \frac{\exp(u\cdot x-t|x|^2/2)}
       {\int\exp(u\cdot y-t|y|^2/2)\dd\lambda^{\otimes M}(y)}.
\]
The finite exponential product has a local exponential moment.  Uniform dominated convergence
as $(a,\bar T)\downarrow(0,0)$ permits $a,\bar T>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \int e^{a|x|}\dd\lambda^{\otimes M}(x)\le2,
  \qquad
  \sup_{|u|\le a,\ 0\le t\le\bar T}
  \norm{F_{u,t}-1}_{L^1(\lambda^{\otimes M})}<\eta.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-l1-stability}
\end{equation}
Indeed, one may dominate uniformly by $1+e^{a_0|x|}$ at a fixed sufficiently small local
moment radius $a_0$, while the unnormalized tilts converge pointwise to one.

Let $\sigma_0$ be the finite weighted perimeter measure of $E_0$.  Choose $R<\infty$ with
$\sigma_0(B_R)\ge P_0/2$, and shrink $a,\bar T$ if necessary so that
\begin{equation}
  aR+\frac{\bar T R^2}{2}\le\log2.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-R}
\end{equation}
The conclusions in \eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-l1-stability} remain valid.  Choose $L\ge1$
with $\Prob(|X^0|\le L)\ge1/2$, and choose
\begin{equation}
  0<T_b\le
  \min\left\{\bar T,\frac{a}{2L},
  \frac{a^2}{8M\log(8M)}\right\}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-Tb}
\end{equation}
Define the base event
\begin{equation}
  G_b=\{|X^0|\le L\}\cap
  \left\{
    \max_{1\le j\le M}\sup_{0\le s\le T_b}|B_s^{0,j}|
    \le\frac{a}{2\sqrt M}
  \right\}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-Gb}
\end{equation}
The reflection principle and a union bound give
\[
  \Prob\left(
    \max_j\sup_{s\le T_b}|B_s^{0,j}|>\frac{a}{2\sqrt M}
  \right)
  \le4M\exp\left(-\frac{a^2}{8MT_b}\right)\le\frac12.
\]
Independence of $X^0$ and $B^0$ yields $\Prob(G_b)\ge1/4$.  On $G_b$,
$|c_s^0|=|sX^0+B_s^0|\le a$ for every $s\le T_b$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-l1-stability} gives
\[
  |\nu_s^0(E_0)-\tfrac12|<\eta.
\]
For $x\in B_R$, equations \eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-l1-stability} and
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-R} also give
\[
  F_{c_s^0,s}(x)
  \ge
  \frac{e^{-aR-T_bR^2/2}}
       {\int e^{a|y|}\dd\lambda^{\otimes M}(y)}
  \ge\frac14.
\]
The exact density-change formula \eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-density-change} therefore gives,
simultaneously for every $0\le s\le T_b$,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_\eta>T_b,
  \qquad
  P_s(E^{M,N})\ge\frac14\sigma_0(B_R)\ge\frac{P_0}{8}
  \quad\hbox{on }G_b.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-base-stability}
\end{equation}
Neither $G_b$ nor $T_b$ depends on the later spectator dimension $N$.

\emph{Step 3: at each fixed time, use a spectator spike to build an exact-mass profile
competitor.}
The published covariance-spike proposition gives, for $N$ independent centered rate-one
exponentials and Gaussian-channel noise variance $s$,
\begin{equation}
  \Prob\left(
    \norm{\Cov(X\mid X+\sqrt sG)}_\op\ge c_{\rm sp}s
  \right)
  \ge1-\left(1-\frac12e^{-s}\right)^N
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-channel}
\end{equation}
for a universal $c_{\rm sp}>0$; see \cite[Prop.~65]{KLnotes}.  Since
\[
  \frac{c_t}{t}=X+\frac{B_t}{t}
  \stackrel{d}=X+t^{-1/2}G,
\]
the exact conversion from localization time to channel noise variance is $s=1/t$.  In the
spectator block set
\[
  H_t=\left\{\max_{1\le i\le N}v_{i,t}\ge\frac{c_{\rm sp}}t\right\}.
\]
The spectator covariance in \eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-factorization} is diagonal, so the
channel event is exactly $H_t$.  Thus, at every deterministic $t>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \Prob(H_t)\ge1-\left(1-\frac12e^{-1/t}\right)^N.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-fixed-time}
\end{equation}
If $\log N\ge2/T$, then for each deterministic $t\in[T/2,T]$ separately,
\begin{equation}
  \Prob(H_t)\ge h_{\rm sp}=1-e^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-fixed-time-positive}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $Ne^{-1/t}\ge Ne^{-2/T}\ge1$ and
$(1-x)^N\le e^{-Nx}$ for $x\in[0,1]$.
This is only a fixed-time statement; the proof never asserts a common spike event on the whole
interval.

On $H_t$, select the least $i$ with $v_{i,t}\ge c_{\rm sp}/t$.  The posterior
$\lambda_{i,t}$ is one-dimensional and log-concave, with a continuous positive density
$f_{i,t}$ in the interior of its support.  The base posterior is equivalent to its prior and
$\lambda^{\otimes M}(E_0)=1/2$, so $0<p_t<1$.  It therefore has a quantile $q_{i,t}$ with
\[
  \lambda_{i,t}(( -\infty,q_{i,t}])=p_t.
\]
The corresponding full-product halfline cylinder has $\mu_t$-mass exactly $p_t$ and perimeter
$f_{i,t}(q_{i,t})$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:one-dimensional-density-variance}, imported from
\cite[Prop.~2.1]{BobkovChistyakov2015Concentration}, yields
\begin{equation}
  I_{\mu_t}(p_t)\le f_{i,t}(q_{i,t})
  \le\norm{f_{i,t}}_\infty
  \le v_{i,t}^{-1/2}
  \le\sqrt{\frac{t}{c_{\rm sp}}}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-profile-competitor}
\end{equation}
This is a pointwise upper bound on the random profile using an explicit exact-mass competitor;
no lower estimate for a moving localized profile is inserted.

\emph{Step 4: choose time, then dimension, and integrate the fixed-time events.}
With the base and $T_b$ already fixed, choose $T>0$ so that
\begin{equation}
  T\le\min\left\{T_0,T_b,\frac{c_{\rm sp}P_0^2}{256},1\right\},
  \qquad
  T^\gamma<\frac{\kappa_{\rm sp}P_0}{4C}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-T}
\end{equation}
Such a positive $T$ exists.  Only now choose a finite integer $N$ satisfying
$\log N\ge2/T$, and set $d=M+N$, $\mu=\mu^{M,N}$, and $E=E^{M,N}$.

For every deterministic $t\in[T/2,T]$, on $G_b\cap H_t$, equations
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-base-stability} and
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-profile-competitor} give
\begin{equation}
  t<\tau_\eta,
  \qquad
  e_t(E)\ge\frac{P_0}{8}-\sqrt{\frac{t}{c_{\rm sp}}}
  \ge\frac{P_0}{16}.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-excess-lower}
\end{equation}
For each such deterministic $t$, the base event $G_b$ and spectator event $H_t$ are
independent, since they depend on disjoint latent and Brownian blocks.  Hence
\[
  \Prob(G_b\cap H_t)\ge\frac{h_{\rm sp}}4.
\]
Since excess is nonnegative, Tonelli applied to these marginal-in-time events gives
\begin{align}
  \E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}e_t(E)\dd t
  &=\int_0^T\E\bigl[e_t(E)\one_{\{t<\tau_\eta\}}\bigr]\dd t
  \notag\\
  &\ge\frac{P_0}{16}\frac{h_{\rm sp}}4\int_{T/2}^T\dd t
  =\kappa_{\rm sp}P_0T.
  \label{eq:sol-spectator-rate-integrated-lower}
\end{align}
No persistence of the spike event and no interchange beyond nonnegative Tonelli is used.

The base selection \eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-epsilon} and the time selection
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-T} imply
\[
  Ce_0(E)<\frac{\kappa_{\rm sp}P_0}{4},
  \qquad
  CT^\gamma<\frac{\kappa_{\rm sp}P_0}{4}.
\]
Consequently
\[
  C\bigl(Te_0(E)+T^{1+\gamma}\bigr)
  <\frac{\kappa_{\rm sp}P_0T}{2}
  <\E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau_\eta}e_t(E)\dd t,
\]
which is \eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-violation}.  The complete quantifier order is
\[
  (C,T_0,\gamma,\eta,\delta)
  \longrightarrow(\varepsilon,M,E_0,P_0,T_b)
  \longrightarrow T
  \longrightarrow(N,d,\mu,E).
\]
In particular, both near-minimality estimates were fixed uniformly in $N$ before $T$ and the
spectator dimension were selected.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Dependency and fence audit.}
The proof uses exactly the four ledger dependencies in the header.  Proposition
\ref{prop:products} and Lemma~\ref{lem:half} keep the monotone half-profile limit uniformly
positive.  Proposition~\ref{prop:covariance-spike} supplies only the separate fixed-time events
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-fixed-time}; Lemma
\ref{lem:one-dimensional-density-variance} supplies the exact-quantile profile competitor.
The node has no \texttt{bounded\_by} edge.  The neighbouring circularity fence is respected
because the proof upper-bounds the random profile by an explicit set instead of assuming a
localized isoperimetric lower bound.  The two-tail obstruction and the trace-upgrade cluster are
not used, identified with this statement, or contradicted.

\paragraph{Compatibility with the existing excess and KLS interfaces.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial-excess} gives the universal upper bound
$\E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau}e_t\dd t\le(1+e_0)T$.  There is no conflict: the lower bound
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-integrated-lower} is itself only of order $T$.  It shows that the
coefficient of this $O(T)$ term cannot uniformly vanish with $e_0$ and $T^\gamma$.  Indeed,
$P_0=I_\mu(1/2)+e_0\le1+e_0$ by the same halfspace comparison used in
Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial-excess}.

The near-worst bootstrap in Theorem~\ref{thm:bootstrap} is also untouched.  This construction
supplies no near-worst-measure premise, and that theorem retains the external measure-scale term
$h_\mu(T^{4/3}+\Xi_T)$ rather than asserting the uniform superlinear remainder refuted here.
Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:products} says precisely that all witness laws satisfy KLS.  KLS
controls their isoperimetry; it does not require the source-vanishing excess-propagation rate in
\eqref{eq:sol-spectator-rate-violation}.

\paragraph{Hypotheses and closure.}
All four dependencies are proved nodes or published imports in the current ledger, so the result
is unconditional relative to the repository's accepted analytic inputs.  The proof additionally
uses standard deterministic weighted-BV approximation and tube formulas, local exponential
integrability, the planted Gaussian posterior representation, the Brownian reflection principle,
and Tonelli's theorem.  No numerical evidence and no weighted-excess inequality enter.  There is
no step left analytically open in this dossier; \texttt{checked\_by: none} records that a distinct
cold reviewer has not yet audited it.

\begin{abstract}
  This is a unified treatment of three functional inequalities --- the Poincar\'e ($C_{\mathrm P}$),
  log-Sobolev ($C_{\mathrm{LS}}$), and quadratic transportation-cost ($C_{\mathrm{TCI}}$) constants
  --- for measures $P\propto e^{-U}$ used in statistics and machine learning, organized as a
  difficulty gradation. \textbf{Part~I} certifies explicit, dimension-controlled constants for
  structured parameterized families by routing each to a classical template (Bakry--\'Emery,
  Brascamp--Lieb, Holley--Stroock, tensorization, Otto--Villani, Hardy/Muckenhoupt): Gaussian and
  log-concave calibration, bounded perturbations and products, the flagship Gaussian-prior
  generalized-linear-model posterior bound $C_{\mathrm P}\le\lambda_{\max}(\Sigma_0)$ (data-free,
  dimension-free), the heavy-tailed obstruction to LSI/$T_2$, and multimodal / hierarchical
  posteriors; it closes with the open research agenda for sharp Bayesian-posterior constants.
  \textbf{Part~II} develops that agenda as A-series results and open-target deep dives, one section
  per flagship problem
  --- data-informed GLM-posterior constants, the Bernstein--von Mises limit, heavy-tailed posteriors
  beyond classical LSI, variational-inference transport constants, and the
  quotient/reparameterization of symmetry- and funnel-induced slow modes. It promotes the
  twenty-one independently reviewed results and sharp model calibrations already obtained, records useful
  obstructions, and separates them from proof drafts and the remaining open targets.
  \textbf{Part~III} maps the Kannan--Lov\'asz--Simonovits frontier --- the dimension-free
  Poincar\'e bound for arbitrary isotropic log-concave measures, the ``Tier-$\infty$'' boundary of
  Part~I. It distinguishes the sharp July 2026 radial and homogeneous-quadratic results from full KLS,
  develops the conditional fixed-cut Eldan program in detail, and records the separate
  fixed-eigenfunction, deterministic moment-map/Haar, and inverse-variance conditional-fiber
  routes with their exact open bottlenecks.
\end{abstract}

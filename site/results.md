**Nothing here proves the conjecture**, and nothing here refutes it: [](#conj:kls) is open.
What follows are the results this project has proved on the way. They are of three kinds,
and each is labelled as such.

- *Unconditional theorems*: a new inequality that implies KLS and the cases where it is
  proved (Theorems A–C), and a ceiling on one strategy (Theorem F).
- *Conditional reductions*: implications whose hypothesis is itself open, or rests on an
  unrefereed preprint. They are proved as implications and are not progress on KLS until
  their hypothesis is (Theorems D and E).
- *Counterexamples* to natural intermediate estimates (Theorems G–I). None of them touches
  KLS: every witness satisfies the conjecture.

Throughout, $\mu$ is a centred log-concave probability measure on $\mathbb R^n$ with
covariance $\Sigma$, and
$C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)=\sup_f\operatorname{Var}_\mu f\,/\,\mathbb E_\mu\langle\Sigma\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle$
is its affine-invariant Poincaré constant. KLS is the statement that
$C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}$ is bounded by a universal constant.

## A single inequality that would imply KLS

The first three results do not use stochastic localization at all. They start from the
*moment map* of Cordero-Erausquin and Klartag
[@CorderoErausquinKlartag2015MomentMeasures]: a centred log-concave $\mu$ is the image of
the measure $e^{-\varphi(y)}\,\mathrm dy$ under $\nabla\varphi$, for an essentially unique
convex $\varphi$. Transported to $\mu$, the Hessian $H=D^2\varphi\circ(\nabla\varphi)^{-1}$
is a *Stein kernel*: $\mathbb E_\mu[\operatorname{Tr}(H\,D^2g)]=\mathbb E_\mu[\langle x,\nabla g\rangle]$
for every test function $g$, and $\mathbb E_\mu H=\Sigma$. It defines a generator
$L_\mu g=\operatorname{Tr}(H D^2g)-\langle x,\nabla g\rangle$, symmetric for $\mu$, which for
the standard Gaussian ($H=\mathrm{Id}$) is the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck operator. The
*moment-Hessian constant* ([](#def:cmh)) is

$$
C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\mu)=\sup_g\frac{\mathbb E_\mu\langle H\nabla g,\Sigma^{-1}H\nabla g\rangle}{\mathbb E_\mu(L_\mu g)^2}.
$$

:::{prf:theorem} The moment-Hessian inequality implies Poincaré
:label: site:thm-a
:enumerator: A
For every centred log-concave $\mu$ with a smooth positive density on a convex body,
$C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)\le C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\mu)$. Consequently a universal bound
$C_{\mathrm{CMH}}\le4$ would prove KLS, with affine Poincaré constant $4$.
:::

**Idea of the proof.** One Cauchy–Schwarz. Given a centred $f$, solve $-L_\mu g=f$ (up to a
spectral truncation). Then
$\|f\|_2^2=\langle f,-L_\mu g\rangle=\mathbb E_\mu\langle\nabla f,H\nabla g\rangle
\le(\mathbb E_\mu\langle\Sigma\nabla f,\nabla f\rangle)^{1/2}
(\mathbb E_\mu\langle H\nabla g,\Sigma^{-1}H\nabla g\rangle)^{1/2}$, and the last factor is at
most $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}^{1/2}\|L_\mu g\|_2=C_{\mathrm{CMH}}^{1/2}\|f\|_2$. Only the symmetry,
the positivity and the Stein identity of $H$ are used, which is why the constant passes with
no loss. The restriction to smooth densities on convex bodies costs nothing for KLS: every
log-concave law is a limit of such measures along which the affine Poincaré constant is
lower semicontinuous ([](#lem:affine-poincare-w2-liminf)).

The converse is not known. A weighted Hodge decomposition splits the numerator into a
gradient part, which is *exactly* the affine Poincaré quotient, and a divergence-free part
which vanishes in dimension one but not beyond; so the moment-Hessian inequality asks for
something more than KLS in dimension $n\ge2$, but whether $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}\le4$ is strictly
stronger than KLS is open ([](#rem:cmh-stronger-than-kls)).

:::{prf:theorem} Where the moment-Hessian inequality is proved
:label: site:thm-b
:enumerator: B
1. On the line, $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\mu)=C_{\mathrm P}(\mu)/\operatorname{Var}(\mu)$ exactly;
   hence $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}\le4$ for every one-dimensional log-concave law, and the
   exponential has constant exactly $4$.
2. The constant of a product is the largest constant of its factors.
3. Every log-concave Dirichlet law — the law of $(\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_m)/\sum_j\gamma_j$
   for independent $\gamma_i\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\alpha_i)$ with all $\alpha_i\ge1$, which
   includes the uniform measure on a simplex — satisfies $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}\le4$, with a
   strict margin once $\sum_i\alpha_i>3$.

In particular every log-concave Dirichlet law, and every product, linear image or
convolution of independent ones, has affine Poincaré constant at most $4$.
:::

**Idea of the proof.** On the line the divergence-free part of Theorem A vanishes and the
inequality becomes an identity. For products, the cross terms
$\mathbb E[(L_ig)(L_jg)]$ between factors are nonnegative, so the denominator only gains.
The Dirichlet case is the first non-product family, and its proof lifts the simplex to the
independent Gamma variables: a function on the simplex is a function of the Gammas that is
homogeneous of degree zero. The Bochner identity of the Gamma product, a completed square,
and the Euler relation that homogeneity imposes reduce the claim to a scalar minimization in
one variable per coordinate, which is where $\alpha_i\ge1$ — log-concavity — is spent.
Dimension-free Poincaré bounds for simplices were known qualitatively
[@KolesnikovMilman2016OrliczKLS]; what is new is the constant $4$ and the stronger
moment-Hessian inequality.

The product case is also a warning. A product of centred exponentials has
$C_{\mathrm{CMH}}=4$ *exactly*, with no slack ([](#rem:cmh-saturation-risk)): the
inequality one would need in general is already tight on a product, so any log-concave
perturbation of that product that increases the constant would refute it. Whether one
exists is open.

:::{prf:theorem} The linear test is a third-moment problem
:label: site:thm-c
:enumerator: C
Let $\mu$ be isotropic and log-concave (with a mild regularity assumption on its moment
map), with Stein kernel $\tau$, and let
$T_3(a)=\mathbb E_\mu[\langle X,a\rangle\,X\otimes X]$ be its directional third moment. For
every unit vector $a$,

$$
\mathbb E_\mu|\tau a|^2=1+\tfrac14\|T_3(a)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\mathbb E_\mu|v_a|^2
$$

for a remainder $v_a$ orthogonal to all affine functions. Hence an operator bound
$\mathbb E_\mu\tau^2\preceq c\,\mathrm{Id}$ forces $\|T_3(a)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le2\sqrt{c-1}$.
Moreover the exponential cone measures with $\beta=n$ attain the value
$\mathbb E|\tau e_1|^2=2$ in their axis direction (in isotropic coordinates), whatever
their base.
:::

**Why this matters.** Testing the moment-Hessian inequality on linear functions gives its
cheapest necessary condition, $\mathbb E_\mu\tau^2\preceq4\,\mathrm{Id}$ in isotropic
position ([](#conj:gate-zero), open). A recent preprint proves the trace of this,
$\operatorname{Tr}\mathbb E\tau^2\le2n$ ([](#thm:chen-klartag-moment-hessian), itself treated
as open here), and the trace bound is attained, so the natural operator statement is the
sharp form $\mathbb E_\mu\tau^2\preceq2\,\mathrm{Id}$ ([](#conj:gate-zero-sharp), open).
Theorem C says what these conjectures contain: the sharp form would give a universal bound
$\|T_3(a)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le2$ on third moments, sharper than the $2\sqrt2$ derived from the
unrefereed preprint described on [the problem page](problem.md) ([](#prop:letwin-kappa)). So proving it is at
least as hard as a sharp third-moment estimate. The exponential cones — density
$\propto x_1^{\beta-n}e^{-x_1}$ on the cone over a centred convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^{n-1}$
— have an explicit moment map, and at $\beta=n$ they saturate the sharp form in the axis
direction whatever $K$ is: they are its first equality cases that are not products. Over a
cube base the whole matrix $\mathbb E\tau^2$ is computed in closed form and never exceeds
$2\,\mathrm{Id}$ ([](#cor:cube-cone-gate-zero)).

**Idea of the proof.** The Stein identity applied to quadratic test functions says that
$\mathbb E_\mu[\tau_{ij}X_k]$ is half the third-moment tensor. Projecting the column $\tau a$
onto the span of $1,X_1,\dots,X_n$ then leaves $a+\tfrac12T_3(a)X$, and Pythagoras gives the
identity. For the cones, the moment potential lifts from that of the base $K$, and the axis
column of the kernel turns out to be the position vector itself, so the axis quotient is an
explicit Gamma moment, $1+n/\beta$.

statements: [](#lem:linear-sector-third-moment), [](#cor:gate-zero-third-moment),
[](#prop:cone-linear-sector)

## Reductions to open estimates

:::{prf:theorem} Following one eigenfunction; conditional
:label: site:thm-d
:enumerator: D
Run stochastic localization on an isotropic log-concave $\mu$ and follow a first
eigenfunction $f$: set $g_t=\operatorname{Cov}_{p_t}(f,X)$,
$H_t=\mathbb E_{p_t}[(f-\mathbb E_{p_t}f)(X-a_t)^{\otimes2}]$ and $A_t=\operatorname{Cov}(p_t)$.
**If** there are universal $T_0,C_0,C_1$ such that, for $t\le T_0$ (on smooth approximants,
uniformly),

$$
\mathbb E\int_0^t\|H_s\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,\mathrm ds\;\le\;C_0t+C_1\,\mathbb E\int_0^t|g_s|^2\,\mathrm ds+\mathbb E\int_0^t2\,g_s^{\top}A_sg_s\,\mathrm ds,
$$

then KLS holds. The hypothesis is open ([](#q:mm-spectral-occupation)).
:::

**Idea of the proof.** The covariance $g_t$ of the eigenfunction with the coordinates obeys
an exact equation, $\mathrm dg_t=H_t\,\mathrm dW_t-A_tg_t\,\mathrm dt$: a source
$\|H_t\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$ and a damping $2g_t^{\top}A_tg_t$, with no inequality spent. The
hypothesis lets the damping cancel the source, Grönwall's lemma bounds how much of $f$ has
been learned by a universal time, and the Brascamp–Lieb bound $C_{\mathrm P}(p_t)\le1/t$ on
the localized measure turns that into a spectral gap for $\mu$. The eigenfunction is the
right object because, unlike the top covariance eigenvalue, it ignores spikes in directions
it does not use (see [the obstacle](#site:obstacle)).

**How far the hypothesis is from known.** Conditional on the unrefereed quadratic Poincaré
inequality ([](#thm:letwin-qcts)), the hypothesis holds with $C_0=34$, $C_1=0$ up to time
$c/\log^2n$, for eigenfunctions with small eigenvalue ([](#prop:mm-window-occupation)). That recovers only
$C_{\mathrm P}\lesssim\log^2n$, weaker than the published $\log n$
([](#thm:klartag-logn)); its interest is that it locates the whole difficulty in one word.
The estimate holds on a window of length $1/\log^2n$; KLS needs it on a window of universal
length.

:::{prf:theorem} Conditional line resampling; conditional
:label: site:thm-e
:enumerator: E
For a direction $\theta$, resample $X\sim\mu$ along the line through $X$ in direction
$\theta$ from its conditional law, and weight the resulting variance of $f$ by the inverse
conditional variance of the line coordinate. Averaging over an isotropic frame of directions
$\rho$ gives a Dirichlet form $\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}$. For log-concave $\mu$,
$\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)\le4\int|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm d\mu$, and linear functions have
$\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(\langle a,x\rangle)=|a|^2$. **If** every isotropic log-concave $\mu$
admits one frame, chosen independently of $f$, with
$\operatorname{Var}_\mu f\le C\,\mathcal D_{\mu,\rho}(f)$ for a universal $C$, then KLS
holds with constant $4C$. The hypothesis is open ([](#q:conditional-fiber-frame)).
:::

**Idea of the proof.** The comparison with the gradient is the sharp one-dimensional
Poincaré inequality $C_{\mathrm P}\le4\operatorname{Var}$ of Theorem B, applied on each
line. The normalization by the conditional variance is chosen so that linear functions carry
exactly their Euclidean energy — the form cannot lose on the functions that define the
covariance. What it may lose on is shown by the natural frame on the simplex, which fails
([Theorem I](#site:thm-i)). That failure does not show on polynomials: on functions of degree
at most two, the form of that frame is at least $(m+2)(m+3)/(5m^2)>1/5$ times the variance,
with equality on $|X|^2-(m-1)$ ([](#lem:fiber-root-degree-two)). The same computation shows
that a polynomial certificate, in the dual formulation of the question, that no frame works
on the simplex must have degree at least three.

## A ceiling on one strategy

:::{prf:theorem} The near-worst bootstrap, and why it cannot close by itself
:label: site:thm-f
:enumerator: F
Let $\mu$ be an isotropic log-concave measure whose Cheeger constant is within a factor
$1+\varepsilon$ of the smallest one in its dimension. For every cut $E$ of mass $\tfrac12$,
the excess of its boundary over the Cheeger line propagates along stochastic localization,
up to explicit error terms, with one quantity as the only interface:

$$
\Xi_T(\mu)=\int_0^T\mathbb E\bigl(\lambda_{\max}(A_t)-1\bigr)_+\,\mathrm dt .
$$

Always $\Xi_T\le1+\log(nT)$ for $1/n\le T\le1$, and the published covariance estimates give
$\Xi_T\le C(1+\log\log n)$. On the other hand, a bound $\Xi_{T_0}\le\kappa T_0$ at a small
universal time $T_0$, for every isotropic log-concave $\mu$, would by itself imply KLS.
:::

**What it teaches.** The comparison is the natural way to feed the worst measure back into
itself. Its last clause is a ceiling: the input that would make it close is already as
strong as the conclusion. Any argument that improves the propagation of excess to a scale
relative to $T$ must bring something the bootstrap does not contain.

**Idea of the proof.** Two deterministic comparisons do the work. The isoperimetric profile
of a log-concave measure is concave and symmetric, so its Cheeger constant is read at mass
$\tfrac12$; and whitening a localized measure of covariance $A_t$ loses at most
$\lambda_{\max}(A_t)^{1/2}$ against the worst isotropic measure of the same dimension.
Near-worstness then compares the localized Cheeger constant to that of $\mu$, and every loss
is charged to $(\lambda_{\max}(A_t)-1)_+$.

## Counterexamples

Each of these refutes a statement that a proof strategy wanted, and each teaches where the
strategy must change. None refutes KLS.

:::{prf:theorem} Independent spectators break global covariance weights
:label: site:thm-g
:enumerator: G
Follow a cut $E$ of mass $\tfrac12$ along stochastic localization and let $e_t(E)$ be the
excess of its boundary over the isoperimetric profile. For all constants $C,T_0,\gamma>0$
there are a dimension, a product of centred exponentials and a cylinder $E$ with arbitrarily
small initial excess such that, for some $T\le T_0$ (before the stopping time $\tau$ at
which the cut becomes unbalanced),

$$
\mathbb E\int_0^{T\wedge\tau}e_t(E)\,\bigl(1+\|A_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr)^{5/2}\,\mathrm dt\;>\;C\bigl(Te_0(E)+T^{1+\gamma}\bigr).
$$

The same holds without the weight $(1+\|A_t\|_{\mathrm{op}})^{5/2}$.
:::

**What it teaches.** This refutes a weighted propagation estimate that a fixed-cut
approach to KLS was built on ([](#q:weighted) is refuted). The mechanism is the covariance
spike described [on the problem page](#site:obstacle): take a good cut in a
few coordinates and add many independent exponential coordinates it does not depend on.
Their spikes inflate the global weight, and they also depress the isoperimetric profile of
the localized measure, while the cut does not notice them. A weight that charges the global
top eigenvalue is therefore unstable under adding independent coordinates — it fails the
tensorization test. Removing the weight is not enough either
([](#prop:spectator-excess-rate-obstruction)): a surviving estimate must also change its
remainder — for instance allow one linear in $T$ — or assume the measure is near-worst, as
Theorem F does.

[Full proof →](#thm:sol-weighted-spectator-obstruction) · Precise statements:
[](#prop:weighted-spectator-obstruction), [](#prop:spectator-excess-rate-obstruction)

:::{prf:theorem} Matrix inequalities alone do not give the linear test
:label: site:thm-h
:enumerator: H
For every $m\ge18$ there is a random positive semidefinite $(m+1)\times(m+1)$ matrix $H$
with $\mathbb EH=\mathrm{Id}$ and $\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(BHBH)\le2\operatorname{Tr}(B^2)$
for every symmetric $B$, yet $\lambda_{\max}(\mathbb EH^2)>4$.
:::

**What it teaches.** The inequality $\mathbb E\operatorname{Tr}(BHBH)\le2\operatorname{Tr}(B^2)$
for every fixed $B$ is what the moment-map preprint proves for the Hessian of the moment map
([](#thm:letwin-moment-map), open here). The countermodel shows that this family of estimates,
one for each fixed matrix, does not imply the operator bound $\mathbb EH^2\preceq4\,\mathrm{Id}$
needed in Theorem C. The countermodel is not claimed to be a moment-map Hessian, so the
conjecture itself is untouched; but a proof must use more of the moment-map structure than
matrix algebra over fixed $B$. It is the fixed-versus-adaptive pattern of
[the problem page](#site:obstacle) once more.

[Full proof →](#prop:sol-countermodel) · [Precise statement →](#prop:letwin-not-gate-zero)

:::{prf:theorem} The natural frame fails on the simplex
:label: site:thm-i
:enumerator: I
On the isotropic uniform simplex of dimension $m-1$, the frame of root directions
$(e_i-e_j)/\sqrt2$ has spectral gap $O(m^{-2})$ for the form of Theorem E: the indicator of
the corner $\{mP_1>m-\varepsilon\}$ has
$\mathcal D(\mathbf 1_A)/\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf 1_A)\le 12(m-1)/\bigl((m+1)(m-\varepsilon)^2(1-(\varepsilon/m)^{m-1})\bigr)$.
:::

**What it teaches.** Resampling along the root direction $(e_i-e_j)/\sqrt2$ redistributes
the barycentric coordinates $P_i,P_j$ with their sum fixed. Only the $m-1$ directions that
involve the vertex can move the corner indicator, and in the normalization of the form each
of them carries a weight of order $m^{-3}$ there, so the corner costs only $O(m^{-2})$ of its
variance — although, by Theorem B, the simplex itself has a dimension-free Poincaré constant.
The frame dictated by the symmetries of the simplex is therefore not the right one, and by
Theorem E a proof that no frame works would need functions of degree at least three.

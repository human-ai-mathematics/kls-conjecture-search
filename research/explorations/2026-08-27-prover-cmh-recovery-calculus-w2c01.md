## Statement proved

For a centered log-concave probability $\mu$ in a declared ambient $\mathbb R^n$, the dossier
defines

$$
\mathfrak R_n(\mu)
=\inf_{(\mu_k)\in\operatorname{Rec}_n(\mu)}
  \liminf_k C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\mu_k),
$$

where every member of every sequence is centered, full-dimensional, compactly supported, and in
the published positive-smooth compact-target moment-map class, and convergence is in the original
ambient $W_2$ metric. It proves:

1. $C_P^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)\le\mathfrak R_n(\mu)$, together with the useful universal floor
   $\mathfrak R_n(\mu)\ge1$ imposed by the full-dimensional recovery convention.
2. Exact invariance under invertible linear maps.
3. One-way monotonicity
   $\mathfrak R_n(T_\#\mu)\le\mathfrak R_n(\mu)$ for a possibly singular square map
   $T:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$, in the same declared ambient dimension.
4. The finite-product upper bound by the maximum of the factor envelopes.
5. Extension of an intrinsic affine-support recovery with CMH liminf at most $C$ to an ambient
   full-dimensional recovery with liminf at most $\max\{C,1\}$.

The exact calibrations then give envelope $1$ for every centered Gaussian, including singular
Gaussians and the ambient point mass; envelope $4$ for every finite product of one-dimensional
log-concave factors containing a centered one-sided exponential; and envelope at most $4$ for
the stated invertible-image, same-ambient singular-square-image, product, and affine-support
closures generated by one-dimensional log-concave laws and uniform-simplex blocks.

The result is unconditional relative to the repository's certified dependencies. It does not
prove a bounded envelope for an arbitrary log-concave target and does not discharge
`ass:cmh-recovery-envelope`.

## Nonattainment and diagonal selection

The proof does not assume that either infimum in the envelope definition is attained. If
$r=\mathfrak R_n(\mu)<\infty$, then for every $\epsilon,\eta>0$ it first chooses a recovery
whose liminf is below $r+\eta/2$ and then selects a sufficiently far member $\nu$ satisfying

$$
W_2(\nu,\mu)<\epsilon,
\qquad
C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\nu)<r+\eta.
$$

The same extraction works from a specified sequence with liminf at most $C$. This one-shot
lemma is used with errors tending to zero in all later constructions.

For a singular square $T$, invertible matrices $T_j\to T$ and independently selected good laws
$\nu_j\to\mu$ give regular full-dimensional finite-stage laws $(T_j)_\#\nu_j$. Their CMH
constants equal those of $\nu_j$ by invertible covariance. Under $L^2$ couplings,

$$
\mathbb E|T_jX_j-TX|^2
\le 2\|T_j\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2\mathbb E|X_j-X|^2
   +2\mathbb E|(T_j-T)X|^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

Thus only ambient $W_2$ convergence is passed to the singular target. No canonical kernel is
transported through $T$, and no continuity of CMH is invoked.

For a finite product, a good member is selected separately for every factor and every diagonal
index. This avoids the false assumption that low-CMH subsequences of unrelated recovery
sequences have common indices. Product couplings yield

$$
W_2^2\!\left(\bigotimes_i\nu_{i,j},\bigotimes_i\mu_i\right)
\le\sum_iW_2^2(\nu_{i,j},\mu_i),
$$

and the certified exact product formula gives the maximum CMH constant.

## Lower bounds and regular endpoint

The certified affine-Poincaré lower-semicontinuity dossier proves the stronger sequential fact
that every centered log-concave ambient-$W_2$ sequence satisfies

$$
C_P^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)
\le\liminf_k C_P^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu_k).
$$

The present dossier recalls why the quantifiers are valid for an arbitrary recovery: covariance,
variance, and covariance energy converge on one common ambient smooth core; one fixes a
subsequence realizing the liminf before choosing test functions; intrinsic core density handles
singular support. The certified regular CMH endpoint then gives, member by member,
$C_P^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu_k)\le C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\mu_k)$.

The separate envelope floor uses the admissible affine test $g_a(x)=a\cdot x$. Weak zero flux
places it in the closed Stein operator domain, $-L g_a=a\cdot x$, and Jensen with
$\mathbb EH=\Sigma$ yields

$$
\mathbb E|\Sigma^{-1/2}Ha|^2
\ge |\Sigma^{-1/2}\mathbb E[H]a|^2
=a^\top\Sigma a
=\mathbb E(Lg_a)^2.
$$

Hence every full-dimensional recovery member has CMH at least one, including recoveries of a
rank-zero target where $C_P^{\mathrm{aff}}=0$.

## Affine-support collapse and the Neumann normal factor

For the standard Gaussian conditioned to $(-R,R)$,

$$
d\zeta_R=Z_R^{-1}e^{-x^2/2}\mathbf 1_{(-R,R)}\,dx,
$$

the exact one-dimensional CMH identity gives
$C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\zeta_R)=C_P(\zeta_R)/\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_R)$. The dossier proves the
hard-boundary Neumann Bakry--Émery estimate directly. For
$A_Rf=-f''+xf'$ with $f'(\pm R)=0$,

$$
\|A_Rf\|_2^2
=\int\big((f'')^2+(f')^2\big)\,d\zeta_R
\ge\langle f,A_Rf\rangle.
$$

The closed nonzero spectrum is therefore contained in $[1,\infty)$ and
$C_P(\zeta_R)\le1$. The coordinate test gives
$C_P(\zeta_R)\ge\operatorname{Var}(\zeta_R)$, while the latter variance tends to one. Thus
$C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\zeta_R)\to1$.

On $\mathbb R^n=S\oplus S^\perp$, good intrinsic members are tensored with

$$
\left((x\mapsto\epsilon_jx)_\#\zeta_{R_j}\right)^{\otimes(n-d)},
\qquad \epsilon_j\downarrow0,quad R_j\uparrow\infty.
$$

Every finite-stage product has a positive smooth density on a full-dimensional compact convex
target. Orthogonal product couplings collapse the normal variance, scaling leaves CMH unchanged,
and the product theorem costs only $\max\{C,1\}$. The zero-dimensional intrinsic case is handled
directly by a product of these scaled normal factors.

## Exact model classes and boundary semantics

- Truncated-Gaussian products recover the standard Gaussian with CMH tending to one. Invertible
  invariance handles nondegenerate Gaussians, and singular-square monotonicity plus the floor
  handles singular Gaussians.
- Every regular one-dimensional log-concave approximant has
  $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}=C_P/\operatorname{Var}\le4$. Tensorization gives the product upper bound.
  A centered one-sided exponential has affine Poincaré constant exactly four, so the envelope
  lower bound makes every all-one-dimensional product containing that factor exactly sharp.
- A centered uniform simplex is itself in the positive-smooth compact-target class intrinsically.
  For $m=2$ its CMH constant is $12/\pi^2$; for $m=3$ the main Dirichlet theorem applies at
  $A=3$ with $s_3=0$ and still gives the non-strict bound four; for $m>3$ the certified surplus
  is positive. No nonuniform Dirichlet law with a face-vanishing density is used.
- Products, invertible intrinsic images, same-ambient singular square limits, and intrinsic
  affine-support embeddings preserve the at-most-four conclusion by the proved operations.
  An embedding into a larger ambient space is handled intrinsically and then by adding normal
  factors, not by a rectangular projection theorem.

All centering, full-dimensionality, compact-target density, product coupling, boundary-flux,
and closed-core conditions are checked at the finite stages where CMH is evaluated.

# Where the Song–Zhang recovery loss enters

## Question examined

Starting lens: `mine`, for the bounded exploratory probe `ap:sz-recovery-probe`.
The question is whether the factor four in `thm:sz-iterated-curvature` can be
reduced by exploiting the tensors actually generated in
`thm:sz-curvature-comparison`. This checkpoint mines those two certified
solutions; it changes neither. All arguments below are *established* in the
checkpoint sense, not independently certified. No candidate is proposed.

The exact comparison assertion being mined is: for every $0<\varepsilon\le1$,
every nondecreasing $\ell:\mathbb N\to[1,\infty)$, every
$R\ge2^{40}\varepsilon^{-2}$, and every centered smooth law $e^{-W}dx$ with
$aI\preceq D^2W\preceq b_0I$ for finite $b_0\ge a>0$ and covariance at most
$I$, if its Appell coefficients satisfy
$c_k\le R^k\ell(k)^k/(k+1)^2$ for every $k\ge1$, then every dyadic $d\ge2$
satisfies
$C_P\le16(1+\varepsilon)R^2\ell(d)^2\max(1,a^{-1/(d+1)})$.
The antecedent and the threshold on $R$ are retained throughout.

## What we learned

### The two different costs of recovery

*Established, algebraic dissection.* Write the dossier's constants as

$$
 B=2\sqrt{L/(L-2)},\qquad A=bB^2,\qquad J=B^4b^{L+1}.
$$

The mechanism is: polynomial duality recovers a partially symmetric tensor;
normalization defects measure the failure of derivative-slot symmetry; a lag
convolution converts their cumulative effect into a fraction of lost mass.

| Step | Actual cost | Hypothesis used |
|---|---|---|
| Two-block recovery | $C_k\le B^k$ | First $k$ slots symmetric, last $Lk$ slots symmetrized; no gradient origin used |
| Dyadic terminal term | $(A\lambda)^{d/2}c_d/B$ | Degrees double, preceding degrees sum to $d-1$, factorials telescope |
| Normalizer propagation | $b^{(a-1)/2}$ for an old swap | Whole-family scalar normalizers frozen under permutations and bounded by $b\lambda$ |
| Defect term at degree $k$ | Constant times $J^{k/2}c_k\lambda^{(k-1)/2}$ | Prior recoveries cost $B^{k-1}$; recovery at this stage costs another $B^k$ |
| Lag absorption | $2\lambda W_*^2<1$ | Nonnegative lag kernel and $X_N\le\lambda P_N$ on a stopped prefix |

Thus the terminal base tends to $A=4$ as $b\downarrow1,L\to\infty$, whereas
the defect base tends to $J=16$ when also $b^{L+1}\to1$. The curvature
comparison constant must dominate the latter base in this proof. Taking its
square root gives the iteration multiplier four. Normalization is adjustable
near one; it is not itself the persistent four. Dyadic rounding in the outer
iteration is absorbed by the flatness of $\ell_r$ and contributes a summable
loss. The nonnegative convolution bound introduces no extra exponential base,
but forces convergence at the already doubled recovery base.

This identifies a specific bottleneck: improving the terminal term alone does
not remove the defect amplification. An improved proof must control the errors
jointly with recovery, or establish a smaller recovery cost on the actual
family, including its errors.

*Established, limit of constant polishing.* The displayed recovery constant is

$$
 C_k^2=\binom{2k}{k}
 \frac{(Lk)_{\underline k}}{((L-1)k)_{\underline k}}.
$$

Even taking $L\to\infty$ leaves $\binom{2k}{k}$. Since the central binomial
coefficient is the largest of $2k+1$ coefficients summing to $4^k$,
$C_k\ge2^k/\sqrt{2k+1}$. Therefore retaining this recovery formula and merely
sharpening its elementary estimates cannot give subexponential recovery.
This observation concerns the formula, not a claim that every extremal
abstract tensor is generated by the operator construction.

### Gradient origin alone does not supply cross-slot symmetry

*Established, exact regular construction.* Let $\rho=e^{-V}dx$ be a centered,
even, smooth, non-Gaussian one-dimensional probability density with uniformly
positive bounded Hessian. An explicit recipe is

$$
 V(x)=x^2/2+\delta\cos x+\log Z,\qquad 0<\delta<1.
$$

Put $s=\mathbb E X^2$ and $I=\mathbb E V''(X)$. Gaussian tails justify
integration by parts, which gives

$$
 \mathbb E[XV'(X)]=1,\qquad
 I=\mathbb E[V'(X)^2],\qquad sI>1.
$$

The strict inequality is Cauchy--Schwarz: equality would make $V'$ proportional
to $x$ almost everywhere, hence everywhere by continuity, contradicting the
nonzero cosine perturbation. Define

$$
 c=\frac{1+sI}{2s},\qquad g(x)=V'(x)-cx,\qquad
 h(x_1,x_2)=g(x_1)x_2
$$

under $\nu=\rho\otimes\rho$. Its gradient is centered by parity and
independence. With $Q_1v=\mathbb E[X\otimes v]$, direct factorization yields

$$
 Q_1\nabla h=\frac{sI-1}{2}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Indeed $\mathbb E[Xg]=(1-sI)/2$ and
$s\mathbb E[g']=(sI-1)/2$; the diagonal entries vanish. Consequently this
nonzero tensor is killed by symmetrization of its two slots.

This really lies in the centered-gradient inverse-square-root range. For the
self-adjoint weighted Laplacian $H$, set $F=H^{1/2}h$. The explicit smooth $h$
and $Hh$ have polynomial growth, so Gaussian tails and smooth cutoffs put
$h\in\operatorname{Dom}(H)$. Thus $F$ is centered and in the form domain,
$H^{-1/2}F=h$, and $DF=P_+\nabla H^{-1/2}F=\nabla h$.
Rescaling both coordinates by the same positive constant makes covariance at
most $I$ if needed and preserves the nonzero skew tensor up to a scalar.
The Hessian remains bounded above and strictly positive below.

The construction is **not** a counterexample to the certified block lemma:
it has only one derivative slot. Nor is $F$ claimed to be a first
eigenfunction, or part of an admissible stopped prefix. It rules out the
shortcut that centered-gradient origin by itself forces favorable cross-slot
symmetry. A stronger claim must retain those additional restrictions.

### Why product first eigenfunctions are an inadequate stress test

*Established, calibration.* In a regular product law the spectrum is the sum
of the coordinate spectra. A first positive eigenfunction lies in the sum of
one-coordinate first eigenspaces: two nonconstant coordinates would have
energy at least twice the minimum positive coordinate eigenvalue. The maps
$H^{-1/2}$, centering, and differentiation preserve this one-coordinate
structure. Hence every generated tensor is supported on pure coordinate
powers. Product Appell factorization shows that pairing with a component
that depends on coordinate $i$ leaves only polynomial slots all equal to $i$.
Thus every $Q_ku^j$ is a sum of tensors
$t_i e_i^{\otimes(k+j+1)}$, and is fully symmetric.

No cross-slot recovery loss is visible on this class, despite the skew example
above arising in the same class with a different starting function. The
starting spectral restriction has mathematical content.

## What resists

Neither mined node has a `bounded_by` edge. The brief's substantive fences
remain: the degree/depth admissibility thresholds cannot be discarded;
uniform exponential Appell coefficients are already equivalent-strength to
`conj:kls` by `prop:sz-exponential-coefficients-equivalence`; and no CMH,
occupation, or trace comparison follows here. The cosine construction has
positive curvature and is not a nonsmooth-boundary substitution.

No estimate has been established on the cross-slot symmetry of a non-product
first-eigenfunction family on the required stopped prefix. In particular there
is no justified quantitative candidate with a recovery base below two.
Removing the persistent multiplier would still leave the independent
$\Gamma\ge Kr^2$ and $R\ge2^{40}\varepsilon^{-2}$ thresholds. This probe has
not discharged either.

## Proposed next step

Retain only a targeted diagnostic, not an admitted KLS route or a universal
candidate. The next explicit calibration is the regular coupled family

$$
 W_\delta(x_1,x_2)=x_1^2+\tfrac32x_2^2+
 \delta\cos(x_1+x_2)+\log Z_\delta,\qquad |\delta|<\tfrac12.
$$

Its Hessian is at least $(2-2|\delta|)I\succeq I$ and bounded above, so
Brascamp--Lieb gives covariance at most $I$. At zero the first eigenvalue is
simple. Follow its first eigenfunction for small nonzero $\delta$, and form
$u^2$ using exactly the dossier's whole-family normalization, whenever the
successors are nonzero. The first degree/block test is
$T=Q_1u^2$, $S=\operatorname{Sym}_3T$ on its three derivative slots. Determine
analytically the leading nonzero terms of
$\|\mathsf P_2S\|^2/\|S\|^2$, the defect $\|T-S\|$, and all preceding
$p_j,\chi_j,\beta_j$. The generic recovery floor for this block is $1/3$.
This tests whether coupling creates the harmful block component already at
the first available block, and whether its size is paid for by defects.

Every report must also check stopped-prefix eligibility. Near a Gaussian the
first gradient can lose almost all its mass, so this calibration may fail the
small-loss prefix condition. Such a failure limits it to a test of tensor
origin and cannot falsify a claim restricted to admissible prefixes. No
numerical output was used in this checkpoint; any future numerical route
decision requires a committed seeded run.

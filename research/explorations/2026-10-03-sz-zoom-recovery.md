# Strategic decision after the first recovery-loss exploration

## Question examined

Does the Song–Zhang mechanism justify a further KLS approach, and how does it
change the priorities among the four existing approaches? This wave opens
`ap:sz-recovery-probe` as a bounded exploration, distinct from the completed
`ap:polynomial-curvature-audit`.

## What we learned

*Established in exploration, not certified as new nodes.* The loss analysis
in [the mining checkpoint](2026-10-03-sz-loss-analysis.md) separates terminal
recovery from recovery-amplified normalization defects. In the existing proof,
their limiting bases differ; tightening the elementary central-binomial
estimate alone cannot remove the persistent multiplier. This identifies a
specific place to seek an improvement, without asserting that the comparison
constant is optimal on its actual spectral tensors.

The same checkpoint supplies an exact regular example showing that membership
in the centered-gradient range does not force cross-slot symmetry. It is not
initialized by a first eigenfunction and does not contain the derivative block
required in the comparison. It therefore excludes only that broad shortcut.
Conversely, product first-eigenfunction families have too much symmetry to
test the harmful components. The independent early critique records the
scope distinctions in
[its checkpoint](2026-10-03-sz-loss-critique.md). These are research records,
not passing certification reports, and no ledger transition follows.

*Strategic judgment.* The new mechanism deserves focused exploratory work.
Its equivalence criterion is not itself a proof method, but equivalence to
KLS is no reason to exclude a formulation that could admit useful estimates.
The source proof gives concrete operators and losses to examine. At present
there is no justified stronger universal recovery estimate to register as a
candidate, so there is no fifth admitted dimension-free approach.

The manuscript's earlier unconditional priority ordering is replaced by
specific structural motivations and a comparison of the four approaches.
For `ap:s-occupation`, the deterministic spectral construction offers a
related mechanism but no estimate for its stochastic source integral. For
`ap:e-trace-upgrade` and the fixed-cut variants, it improves the general
benchmark without curing orientation or spectator problems. The moment-map
approaches still need their own CMH and commutator estimates; the fiber
approaches still need a frame and its form gap. No implication or blocker
has changed status. The certified polynomial-curvature interfaces remain
available to any of these approaches if a further bridge is actually proved.

## What resists

The missing estimate must exploit tensors generated from a first eigenfunction
on a sufficiently long prefix with small cumulative centering loss, and must
retain the defect budget. Generic algebraic sharpness does not settle that
restricted problem. Neither does a favorable product example. A coupled
near-Gaussian calibration can reveal generated asymmetry, but its initial
mass loss can make it ineligible for the comparison's stopped prefix. The
vanishing Gaussian successor also prevents naive differentiation of the
normalization. Independently, the degree/depth initialization and comparison
thresholds still have to be paid.

## Proposed next step

Retain `ap:sz-recovery-probe` as active, with the concrete coupled-potential
test in its portfolio `next`. Use the explicit family at the end of the mining
checkpoint, whose curvature already bounds its covariance, to avoid an
unnecessary whitening change. Record recovery, symmetry error, normalization
defects and prefix eligibility together. The test can diagnose tensor origin;
an ineligible prefix cannot refute a claim restricted to admissible prefixes.

Admission as a new KLS approach requires a precise intermediate estimate, a
mechanism to prove it, a comparison with the existing obstructions and a
discriminating test. A reduced numerical multiplier alone is insufficient.
The current wave ends with this narrowed test and the revised manuscript,
not with an asserted improvement of the Poincare bound.

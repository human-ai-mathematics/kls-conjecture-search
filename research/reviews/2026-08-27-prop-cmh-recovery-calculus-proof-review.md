### Nonattainment and the universal lower bounds

The one-shot lemma handles both possible failures of attainment. For finite
$r=\mathfrak R_n(\mu)$, an envelope competitor with liminf below $r+\eta/2$ exists by the
definition of infimum; an arbitrarily far member lies below that liminf plus $\eta/2$ and can be
chosen after the $W_2$ error is below $\epsilon$. The same quantifier order works for a specified
sequence with liminf at most $C$. Taking $\epsilon=\eta=j^{-1}$ therefore produces a single
recovery sequence with CMH limsup at most $r$, without assuming either infimum or liminf is
attained.

For a finite-stage regular law, an affine function is the restriction of an ambient compactly
supported smooth function equal to it near the compact target. The global weak Stein identity
therefore places it in the closed operator domain and gives $-L_\nu(a\cdot x)=a\cdot x$. With
$\Sigma\succ0$ and $\mathbb EH=\Sigma$, the denominator is $a^\top\Sigma a$, while Hilbert-space
Jensen gives

$$
 \mathbb E\lvert\Sigma^{-1/2}Ha\rvert^2
 \ge \lvert\Sigma^{-1/2}\mathbb E[H]a\rvert^2
 =a^\top\Sigma a.
$$

Thus every finite-stage CMH constant is at least one and so is every nonzero-ambient envelope,
including the envelope of a point-mass target. Independently, the certified sequential affine-
Poincare lower semicontinuity and the certified regular endpoint give, for every recovery sequence,

$$
 C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu)
 \le\liminf_k C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}(\mu_k)
 \le\liminf_k C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\mu_k).
$$

Taking the infimum is valid in the extended reals. No CMH limit or semicontinuity is used.

### Linear covariance and the singular-square limit

For invertible $T$, transformed finite-stage targets retain positive smooth log-concave densities,
compact convex support, centering, and full dimension. The exact canonical identities
$\Sigma_{T_\#\nu}=T\Sigma_\nu T^\top$ and
$H_{T_\#\nu}(Tx)=TH_\nu(x)T^\top$, together with the pullback bijection of the closed operator
domains, leave both CMH numerator and denominator unchanged. The $W_2$ Lipschitz estimate gives
one envelope inequality and $T^{-1}$ gives the other.

For a possibly singular square $T$, the proof correctly assumes the source envelope finite, chooses
independently optimized regular laws $\nu_j$, and chooses invertible $T_j\to T$. Every finite-stage
map is invertible and hence preserves the canonical CMH constant. Since $\|T_j\|_{\rm op}$ is
bounded and the target has finite second moment,

$$
 \mathbb E\lvert T_jX_j-TX\rvert^2
 \le 2\|T_j\|_{\rm op}^2\mathbb E\lvert X_j-X\rvert^2
   +2\mathbb E\lvert(T_j-T)X\rvert^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

This produces an ambient-$W_2$ recovery of $T_\#\mu$ with limsup at most the source envelope. No
kernel is pushed through $T$, and the proof has no rectangular-dimension conclusion.

### Products and independently selected good members

When all factor envelopes are finite, the proof applies the one-shot lemma separately for every
pair $(i,j)$. Thus the good law for factor $i$ at stage $j$ is selected from that factor's own
recovery and does not require low-CMH indices of unrelated sequences to coincide. Because there
are only finitely many factors, the product law is centered, full-dimensional, log-concave, and a
positive-smooth compact-target regular moment-map law; its potential is the sum of the factor
potentials and its canonical data are block diagonal. Product couplings give the displayed sum of
squared $W_2$ errors, and the certified exact product theorem gives the maximum of the separately
selected CMH constants. If one factor envelope is infinite, the asserted extended-real inequality
is indeed vacuous.

### Truncated Gaussians and affine-support normal collapse

The symmetric Gaussian conditioned to $(-R,R)$ is centered and belongs to the declared regular
class because its density is the restriction of a globally positive smooth log-concave function.
The certified one-dimensional identity gives
$C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\zeta_R)=C_{\mathrm P}(\zeta_R)/v_R$. For the ordinary Neumann generator
$A_R=-\partial_{xx}+x\partial_x$, direct weighted integration by parts for an operator-core
function with $f'(\pm R)=0$ gives

$$
 \|A_Rf\|_2^2=\int_{-R}^R\bigl((f'')^2+(f')^2\bigr)\,d\zeta_R
 \ge \langle f,A_Rf\rangle.
$$

The closed Neumann operator is nonnegative with constants as kernel, so spectral projection onto
any subinterval of $(0,1)$ would contradict this inequality. Its nonzero spectrum is therefore in
$[1,\infty)$ and $C_{\mathrm P}(\zeta_R)\le1$. Testing the form with $x$ gives
$C_{\mathrm P}(\zeta_R)\ge v_R$, while conditional second moments give $v_R\to1$. Hence
$C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(\zeta_R)\to1$, and nonzero scaling preserves it by the already checked
invertible covariance.

For a proper $d$-dimensional affine support, $q=n-d\ge1$. The proof selects intrinsic laws with
simultaneously small $W_2$ error and CMH constant below $C+j^{-1}$, tensors them with $q$
independent scaled truncated-Gaussian factors whose CMH constants are below $1+j^{-1}$, and uses
the exact product theorem. Every product is centered, full-dimensional in the declared ambient
space, log-concave, and a positive-smooth compact-target law. The normal coupling has exact cost
$q\epsilon_j^2v_{R_j}\to0$; no relation such as $\epsilon_jR_j\to0$ is needed. This proves the
cost $\max\{C,1\}$. At intrinsic dimension zero the explicit $n$-fold normal product recovers
$\delta_0$ with constant tending to one. If the dossier's `S\subset\mathbb R^n` notation is read
as also allowing $S=\mathbb R^n$, the conclusion is immediate from the selected intrinsic
recovery with no normal factor; the certified manuscript statement itself is the proper-support
case.

### Gaussian, exponential-product, and simplex calibrations

The truncated-Gaussian products converge to the standard Gaussian in product $W_2$ and have CMH
constant tending to one. The universal floor gives equality; invertible covariance handles every
nondegenerate centered Gaussian, and same-ambient singular-square monotonicity plus the floor
handles every singular covariance, including zero.

For every nondegenerate centered one-dimensional log-concave factor, the certified regular
recovery and the exact one-dimensional theorem give memberwise CMH at most four. A degenerate
factor is a point mass with envelope one. Product recovery gives the upper bound four, while
ordinary variance tensorization gives

$$
 C_{\mathrm P}^{\mathrm{aff}}\!\left(\bigotimes_i\rho_i\right)
 =\max_i\frac{C_{\mathrm P}(\rho_i)}{\operatorname{Var}(\rho_i)}
$$

over nondegenerate factors. A centered one-sided-exponential factor makes this maximum exactly
four, so the already checked affine-Poincare lower bound supplies the matching envelope lower
bound.

The centered uniform law $U_m$ on an $(m-1)$-simplex is itself a full-dimensional intrinsic
compact-target regular law: its density is a positive constant smooth function. For $m=2$, the
Neumann interval spectrum gives $C_{\mathrm{CMH}}(U_2)=12/\pi^2<4$. For $m=3$, the certified
Dirichlet theorem applies at $A=3$; its angular surplus is exactly zero, but its non-strict estimate
still gives CMH at most four. For $m>3$ the same main theorem suffices, and the separately
certified surplus makes the optional strengthening strict. Products, invertible maps,
same-ambient singular square maps, and intrinsic extension with vanishing normal factors now give
exactly the stated four-closed class. No nonuniform Dirichlet boundary law is used.

some purely algebraic closure substeps, but it is used for recovery nonemptiness and the model

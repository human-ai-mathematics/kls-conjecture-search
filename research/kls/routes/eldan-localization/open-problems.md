# Eldan route — active handoff

The ledger owns the exact statements. This brief records only the attack surface and acceptance
criteria. Check [`../../obstructions.md`](../../obstructions.md) before starting.

## `q:upgrade` — operator-to-trace occupation

Upgrade `cor:per-direction` from fixed quadratic forms to an absorptive trace estimate for every
balanced fixed cut. `cor:away-from-zero` confines the genuine difficulty to small time.

Start from the covariance-window incidence of the whitened color Hessian, with `q:alignment` as
the product stress test. A proof must use cut-aware information and respect `obs:proj-ceiling` and
`obs:two-tail`; a static effective-rank bound is not the target.

Acceptance: a uniform estimate that directly discharges `ass:all-cut-carleson`, with every source
and damping term matched to `thm:scalar-riccati`.

## `q:weighted` — weighted excess propagation

The unweighted bootstrap is already proved. The missing estimate couples the perimeter excess to
rare covariance inflation with the calibrated $(1+\|A_t\|)^{5/2}$ weight.

Acceptance: the ledger statement's rate uniformly for balanced near-Cheeger cuts, without using a
localized profile lower bound circularly. `obs:two-tail` and the `obs:circularity` warning apply.

## `q:stein-weighted` — stable Stein trace

Intrinsic quadratic-chaos control is available conditional on the Letwin preprint. The missing
step is a localization-uniform almost-stability trace theorem, modulo Jacobi zero modes and with
weighted Reilly boundary terms controlled.

Acceptance: the exact weighted input required by `ass:weighted-package`, including domains,
boundary conventions, and an independently reviewed analytic proof.

## `q:taming` and `q:splitting` — geometric consumption

`q:taming` asks for covariance control at the time supplied by the bootstrap. `q:splitting` asks
for a quantitative almost-splitting theorem from small boundary index form. Their naive coupling
has a type mismatch: the covariance functional is cut-free while splitting is cut-indexed.

Acceptance: first write an explicit bridge object carrying both indices. Do not infer taming from
product structure alone; coordinate covariance spikes survive in split models.

## `q:alignment` — product incident-high residue

The fixed single-coordinate counterexample is refuted by `thm:budget` and `cor:refutation`. The
remaining question is whether a high-complexity balanced cut can spend many coordinate budgets in
one short interval while underdamped.

Acceptance: a uniform analytic incident-high bound or a certified counterexample. The existing
`kls-align` run covers one finite tail-union family and is directional only.

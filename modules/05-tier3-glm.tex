\section{Tier 3 --- the GLM-posterior spine (flagship)}
\label{sec:tier3}

The flagship target, and the first \emph{realistic} statistical family: the posterior of a
generalized linear model under a Gaussian prior. In ML and Bayesian statistics the relevant
measure is $\pi(\theta)\propto e^{-U(\theta)}$ with $U=\text{loss}+\text{regularizer}$; the
GLM posterior is the canonical convex instance.

\begin{family}[Gaussian-prior GLM posterior]
\label{fam:glm-posterior}
Given data $(x_i,y_i)_{i=1}^n$ with covariates $x_i\in\R^D$, a per-sample loss
$\ell(y,\cdot)$ \emph{convex} in the linear predictor (logistic, probit, Poisson, softmax,
\dots), and a Gaussian prior $N(0,\Sigmaz)$, the posterior is
\[
  \pi(\theta\mid y)\ \propto\ \exp\!\Big(-\underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^n\ell(y_i,x_i^\top\theta)
  +\tfrac12\theta^\top\Sigmaz^{-1}\theta}_{=:\,U(\theta)}\Big),\qquad\theta\in\R^D .
\]
\end{family}

\begin{theorem}[GLM posteriors are functional-inequality-good, data-free]
\label{thm:glm-fi}
For the posterior of Family~\ref{fam:glm-posterior},
\[
  \CP\ \le\ \CLS\ \le\ \lmax(\Sigmaz),
  \qquad
  \CTCI\ \le\ \lmax(\Sigmaz),
\]
\emph{independent of the data $X=(x_i)$ and of the dimension $D$}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Hessian of the potential splits into prior and likelihood parts:
\[
  \Hess U(\theta)\ =\ \Sigmaz^{-1}\ +\ \sum_{i=1}^n \ell''(x_i^\top\theta)\,x_ix_i^\top .
\]
Convexity of $\ell(y,\cdot)$ in the linear predictor gives $\ell''\ge0$, so the likelihood
term is a sum of PSD rank-one matrices and
$\Hess U(\theta)\succeq\Sigmaz^{-1}\succeq\lmax(\Sigmaz)^{-1}I_D$ for all $\theta$. Thus $U$
is $m$-strongly convex with floor $m=\lmax(\Sigmaz)^{-1}$. Bakry--\'Emery
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bakry-emery}) yields $\CP\le\CLS\le1/m=\lmax(\Sigmaz)$, and Otto--Villani
(Theorem~\ref{thm:otto-villani}) $\CTCI\le\lmax(\Sigmaz)$. The data enter only through the
discarded PSD term, so the bound is data-free; it involves $\Sigmaz$ but not $D$, so it is
dimension-free.
\end{proof}

\noindent The result is elementary once written --- a curvature-floor reading of
Bakry--\'Emery --- yet it is exactly the kind of explicit, dimension-controlled constant the
program exists to certify, on a family practitioners actually sample (logistic and Poisson
regression, multinomial/softmax with $L^2$ regularization, probit under log-concavity). The
likelihood contributes positive curvature, but the \emph{guaranteed} floor comes from the
Gaussian prior.

\begin{proposition}[The prior is essential]
\label{prop:flat-prior-nonintegrable}
With an improper/flat prior and separable or collinear data, the GLM posterior is
non-integrable --- the MLE escapes to infinity --- so there is no spectral gap and no
Poincar\'e constant. Even with a proper Gaussian prior, the per-sample curvature
$\ell''(x_i^\top\theta)$ vanishes where $\abs{x_i^\top\theta}\to\infty$ (logistic:
$\ell''=\sigma(1-\sigma)\to0$), so the global Hessian floor $\inf_\theta\lmin(\Hess U)$ is set
by the prior alone, $\Sigmaz^{-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This is the flat-direction obstruction. Its consequence is that the bound
$\lmax(\Sigmaz)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:glm-fi} is prior-only \emph{on purpose}, and any attempt
to sharpen it must be a bulk argument, not a global floor.

\begin{question}[Data-informed sharpening]
\label{q:glm-data-informed}
Improve Theorem~\ref{thm:glm-fi} so the certified constant \emph{tracks the data}. The floor
$\Sigmaz^{-1}$ is attained only where $\ell''(x_i^\top\theta)\to0$, i.e.\ in tail directions;
in the likelihood-dominated bulk the posterior concentrates far more than $\lmax(\Sigmaz)$
suggests:
\[
  \CP\ \approx\ \lmax\!\Big(\big(\Sigmaz^{-1}+\textstyle\sum_i\ell''(x_i^\top\hat\theta)\,x_ix_i^\top\big)^{-1}\Big)\ \ll\ \lmax(\Sigmaz).
\]
Prove an upper bound interpolating the prior-dominated and likelihood-dominated regimes.
\end{question}

\noindent This is the genuinely open content (developed in Section~\ref{sec:agenda}). By
Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-prior-nonintegrable} a global Hessian floor cannot close the gap;
the candidate routes are a Lyapunov / local-curvature argument (strong convexity on a compact
high-probability region plus a drift estimate outside it; see
\cite{BakryCattiauxGuillin2008,CattiauxGuillinWangWu2009}) or Brascamp--Lieb
(Theorem~\ref{thm:brascamp-lieb}) with the spatially varying Hessian. The deliverable is a
computable sharpness ratio $\lmax(\Sigmaz)/\lmax(\Cov_\pi)$, validated against the linear-test
lower bound (Remark~\ref{rem:rayleigh-lower-only}) from posterior samples.

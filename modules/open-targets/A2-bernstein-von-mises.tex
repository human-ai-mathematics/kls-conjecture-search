\section{A2 --- Asymptotic constants via Bernstein--von Mises}
\label{sec:a2}

This is the deep-dive companion to the A2 paragraph of the agenda (Section~\ref{sec:agenda}).
The target is to convert the oldest structural fact of parametric Bayesian statistics --- the
asymptotic normality of the posterior --- into sharp asymptotics for the three
functional-inequality constants. The slogan version,
\[
  \pi_n\approx N\!\big(\hat\theta_n,(nI(\theta_0))^{-1}\big)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \CP(\pi_n)\approx\CLS(\pi_n)\approx\CTCI(\pi_n)\approx\tfrac1n\,\lmax\!\big(I(\theta_0)^{-1}\big),
\]
is correct for the Gaussian Laplace approximation itself (Family~\ref{fam:gaussian-covariance})
but is \emph{not} a corollary of the Bernstein--von Mises (BvM) theorem as usually stated. The
purpose of this section is to make the target precise, to isolate exactly where the naive
implication fails, to record the reviewed logistic Poincar\'e route and the exact global
LSI/$T_2$ obstruction, and to separate those results from the broader asymptotic classification
that remains open.

\paragraph{Reviewed status.}
The vanishing-leverage logistic Poincar\'e implication below is the A2 specialization of the
reviewed A1 mode-leverage theorem. Propositions~\ref{prop:a2-subquadratic-global}
and~\ref{prop:a2-logistic-global} have independently reviewed proofs. The general
strong-Laplace transfer, Theorem~\ref{thm:a2-target}, remains an analytic proof draft pending the
repository review step.

\subsection{Setup, scaling, and the precise target}
\label{subsec:a2-setup}

Fix the dimension $d$ throughout (the growing-dimension regime is deferred to
Question~\ref{q:a2-highdim}). Let $\theta_0$ be an interior point of the parameter space, the
data-generating truth, and write the (negative log-)posterior as
\[
  \pi_n(\dd\theta)\ \propto\ e^{-V_n(\theta)}\,\dd\theta,
  \qquad
  V_n(\theta)\ =\ \sum_{i=1}^n \ell_i(\theta)\ -\ \log\rho(\theta),
\]
with $\ell_i$ the per-observation negative log-likelihood and $\rho$ a prior density positive
and smooth near $\theta_0$. Let $\hat\theta_n=\arg\min_\theta V_n(\theta)$ (the MAP), let
$H_n=\Hess V_n(\hat\theta_n)$ be the posterior precision at the mode, and let $I(\theta_0)$ be
the Fisher information, so that under standard regularity $\tfrac1n H_n\to I(\theta_0)$ in
$\Prob_{\theta_0}$-probability. The classical theorem of Bernstein and von Mises
\cite{VanDerVaart1998,GhosalVanDerVaart2017} states that, after the local rescaling
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-rescaling}
  z\ =\ \sqrt n\,(\theta-\hat\theta_n),
  \qquad
  \tilde\pi_n\ =\ \mathrm{law\ of}\ z\ \mathrm{under}\ \pi_n,
\end{equation}
the rescaled posterior converges to a fixed Gaussian \emph{in total variation},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-bvm}
  \big\|\tilde\pi_n-N\!\big(0,I(\theta_0)^{-1}\big)\big\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\ \xrightarrow{\ \Prob_{\theta_0}\ }\ 0 .
\end{equation}

The three constants scale cleanly under \eqref{eq:a2-rescaling}. If $g(z)=f(\hat\theta_n+z/\sqrt n)$
then $\Var_{\pi_n}(f)=\Var_{\tilde\pi_n}(g)$ while $\nabla_z g=n^{-1/2}\nabla_\theta f$, so testing
Definition~\ref{def:poincare} on both sides gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-scaling}
  \CP(\pi_n)=\tfrac1n\,\CP(\tilde\pi_n),\qquad
  \CLS(\pi_n)=\tfrac1n\,\CLS(\tilde\pi_n),\qquad
  \CTCI(\pi_n)=\tfrac1n\,\CTCI(\tilde\pi_n).
\end{equation}
The $1/n$ is therefore not the content; the content is the limit of the \emph{rescaled}
constants. The target is the following.

\begin{conjecture}[Asymptotic functional-inequality constants]
\label{conj:a2}
Under regularity conditions strong enough to control the posterior tails uniformly (made
explicit in Theorem~\ref{thm:a2-target} below), for a regular parametric model in fixed
dimension $d$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-target}
  n\,\CP(\pi_n)\ \xrightarrow{\ \Prob_{\theta_0}\ }\ \lmax\!\big(I(\theta_0)^{-1}\big)
  \ =\ \frac{1}{\lmin\!\big(I(\theta_0)\big)} ,
\end{equation}
and, under additional global hypotheses (Subsection~\ref{subsec:a2-lsi}), the same limit holds
for $n\,\CLS(\pi_n)$ and $n\,\CTCI(\pi_n)$.
\end{conjecture}

Equivalently, by \eqref{eq:a2-scaling}, the claim is that the rescaled constants converge to
those of the limiting Gaussian, $\CP(\tilde\pi_n)\to\CP(N(0,I^{-1}))=\lmax(I(\theta_0)^{-1})$,
and likewise for $\CLS,\CTCI$ --- i.e.\ the BvM Gaussian computes the constants, not merely the
distribution.

\subsection{Why BvM in total variation is not enough}
\label{subsec:a2-tv}

The constants are far more sensitive than total-variation distance: they see tails, remote
mass, gradients, and global geometry, none of which TV controls. Convergence
\eqref{eq:a2-bvm} does not by itself control even posterior covariances, let alone
functional-inequality constants \cite{KasprzakGiordanoBroderick2025}.

\begin{warning}[A TV-BvM sequence with diverging constants]
\label{warn:a2-tv-fails}
Consider the contamination sequence on $\R$
\[
  \mu_n\ =\ (1-\eps_n)\,N(0,1)\ +\ \eps_n\,N(a_n,1),
  \qquad \eps_n\to0,\quad \eps_n a_n^2\to\infty .
\]
Then $\|\mu_n-N(0,1)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le\eps_n\to0$, so $\mu_n$ satisfies a BvM-type limit in TV.
Yet its variance is $\Var_{\mu_n}(x)=1+\eps_n a_n^2-\eps_n^2a_n^2\to\infty$, and the linear test
$f(x)=x$ gives the lower bound $\CP(\mu_n)\ge\Var_{\mu_n}(x)\to\infty$
(Remark~\ref{rem:rayleigh-lower-only}). The same remote sliver of mass inflates $\CLS$ and
$\CTCI$. Hence \eqref{eq:a2-bvm} alone is consistent with all three constants diverging.
\end{warning}

The lesson is structural: to extract constants one needs a \emph{strong} Laplace/BvM statement
that adds (i) a uniform local quadratic approximation of $V_n$ near $\hat\theta_n$ and (ii)
tail / no-bottleneck control ruling out remote mass of the kind in
Warning~\ref{warn:a2-tv-fails}. The clean device for (ii) is bounded perturbation: if the
rescaled posterior is a globally $o_P(1)$-oscillation reweighting of its BvM Gaussian, then
Holley--Stroock (Theorem~\ref{thm:holley-stroock}) transfers the Gaussian constants with a
multiplicative error $e^{\osc}=1+o_P(1)$, for $\CP$ and $\CLS$ simultaneously. The whole
difficulty of A2 is to discharge (i)--(ii) under hypotheses weak enough to cover real models
yet strong enough to defeat Warning~\ref{warn:a2-tv-fails}.

\subsection{The Poincar\'e half is within reach: low-temperature spectral gaps}
\label{subsec:a2-poincare}

Write the posterior as a low-temperature Gibbs measure. With $t=1/n$ and
$f_n(\theta)=\tfrac1n V_n(\theta)\to\ell(\theta)$ (the population risk, minimized at $\theta_0$
with $\Hess\ell(\theta_0)=I(\theta_0)$),
\[
  \pi_n(\dd\theta)\ \propto\ \exp\!\big(-f_n(\theta)/t\big)\,\dd\theta,
  \qquad t=1/n\to0 .
\]
This is exactly the object of low-temperature functional-inequality theory. Chewi and Stromme
\cite{ChewiStromme2024Ballistic} prove that for $\mu_t\propto e^{-f/t}$ with a unique
nondegenerate global minimizer $x_\star$, a finite inverse global PL constant
$C_{\mathrm{PL}}(f)<\infty$ (equivalently a positive rate $\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(f)>0$), the stated
smoothness and Laplacian/gradient-growth bound, and normalizability,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-cs-poincare}
  \lim_{t\to0}\frac{\CP(\mu_t)}{t}\ =\ \frac{1}{\lmin\!\big(\Hess f(x_\star)\big)} ,
\end{equation}
the inverse smallest Hessian eigenvalue at the minimizer --- a purely \emph{local} quantity.
When the population risk $\ell$ satisfies all of these global hypotheses, substituting
$f=\ell$, $x_\star=\theta_0$, $t=1/n$ reproduces the right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:a2-target} term for term. A unique nondegenerate minimizer alone is not enough; for
example, ordinary logistic population risks can have zero global PL rate.

\begin{remark}[What transfer to the random posterior still requires]
\label{rem:a2-transfer}
Equation~\eqref{eq:a2-cs-poincare} is a deterministic statement about a fixed potential $f$.
The posterior potential $f_n=\tfrac1nV_n$ is \emph{random} (empirical-likelihood fluctuations),
its minimizer $\hat\theta_n$ is random and only converges to $\theta_0$, and the prior
contributes an $O(1/n)$ perturbation. Turning \eqref{eq:a2-cs-poincare} into
\eqref{eq:a2-target} therefore needs the low-temperature theorem in a form uniform over a
shrinking neighbourhood of potentials around $\ell$, plus a global tail bound excluding remote
posterior mass (the Warning~\ref{warn:a2-tv-fails} pathology). Related low-temperature toolkits
that could supply the global control are Lyapunov-potential criteria
\cite{ChenSridharan2024Lyapunov}, local log-Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz Poincar\'e bounds
\cite{GongHeShen2025LogPL}, and the constrained-Gibbs posterior Poincar\'e inequalities of
\cite{ConstrainedGibbs2026}, which already prove $O(1/n)$ local Poincar\'e constants on a
high-probability good set for logistic regression, Poisson linear models, and Gaussian mixtures.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The log-Sobolev half is genuinely more delicate}
\label{subsec:a2-lsi}

This is where the naive paragraph is most misleading. For a Gaussian $\CLS=\CP$
(Family~\ref{fam:gaussian-covariance}), so the local-Hessian answer is correct in the
Gaussian-perturbative regime. But for a non-Gaussian low-temperature Gibbs measure the
log-Sobolev constant depends on the \emph{global} landscape, not only on the curvature at the
minimizer. The companion theorem of \cite{ChewiStromme2024Ballistic} identifies the ballistic
limit of the log-Sobolev constant with the \emph{Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz} (PL) constant of $f$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-cs-lsi}
  \lim_{t\to0}\frac{\CLS(\mu_t)}{t}\ =\ \frac{1}{\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(f)},
  \qquad
  \tfrac12\norm{\nabla f(x)}^2\ \ge\ \mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(f)\,\big(f(x)-\min f\big)\ \ \forall x,
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(f)$ is the largest valid PL constant. Since
$\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(f)\le\lmin(\Hess f(x_\star))$ in general, with equality only when the global
landscape is as favourable as the local curvature, one has
\[
  \lim_{t\to0}\frac{\CLS(\mu_t)}{t}\ \ge\ \lim_{t\to0}\frac{\CP(\mu_t)}{t},
\]
consistent with $\CP\le\CLS$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:lsi-implies-poincare}), and the inequality is
\emph{strict} whenever the landscape away from $\theta_0$ is shallow relative to $I(\theta_0)$.

\begin{warning}[The LSI limit need not be Fisher-local]
\label{warn:a2-lsi}
Consequently the clean statement
\[
  n\,\CLS(\pi_n)\to\lmax(I(\theta_0)^{-1})
\]
is valid only in special regimes --- when the posterior is a global $o_P(1)$-oscillation
perturbation of its BvM Gaussian (Holley--Stroock with vanishing oscillation), or when the
negative log-likelihood is globally strongly convex / PL with constant matching $I(\theta_0)$.
For a fixed deterministic potential satisfying the Chewi--Stromme global hypotheses, their
theorem instead gives
\(
  \CLS(\mu_t)/t\to 1/\mu_{\mathrm{PL}}(\ell)
\)
for $\mu_t\propto e^{-\ell/t}$.
This quantity need not equal $\lmax(I(\theta_0)^{-1})$. Transferring that formula to the random,
$n$-dependent posterior potential requires a uniform low-temperature theorem and global tail
control; without them the posterior LSI scale may instead be tail-governed or divergent. Multiple
or shallow remote near-minimizers can change the LSI behaviour even when the BvM Gaussian is
unchanged \cite{BenNejma2026Lojasiewicz}.
\end{warning}

\subsection{Transport follows from a sharp LSI, not from BvM}
\label{subsec:a2-transport}

The Talagrand $T_2$ constant has a clean conclusion in the sharp-LSI regime, but requires its own
analysis outside it. Whenever the log-Sobolev comparison is sharp,
$n\,\CLS(\pi_n)\to\lmax(I^{-1})$, Otto--Villani
(Theorem~\ref{thm:otto-villani}) gives the matching upper bound
$n\,\CTCI(\pi_n)\le\lmax(I^{-1})(1+o_P(1))$, while the implication
$T_2(C)\Rightarrow\Cov\preceq CI$ supplies the reverse inequality under enough moment
control, so $n\,\CTCI(\pi_n)\to\lmax(I(\theta_0)^{-1})$. Outside the Gaussian-perturbative
regime these upper and lower bounds do not identify the $T_2$ asymptotic: it may degrade, but
equality with the LSI scale requires a separate argument. A Fisher-local $T_2$ conclusion here is
a consequence of a \emph{sharp} LSI comparison plus covariance control, never of TV-BvM alone.

\subsection{A strong sufficient transfer theorem (analytic draft)}
\label{subsec:a2-theorem}

The honest statement is not ``BvM implies the constants'' but a strong-Laplace statement that
adds the missing tail and perturbation control. The implication and proof below are an analytic
draft; repository certification requires the separate solution/review step.

\begin{theorem}[Strong-Laplace transfer --- analytic proof draft]
\label{thm:a2-target}
Let $d$ be fixed, $\theta_0$ interior, the prior density positive and smooth near $\theta_0$,
and the model regular with $\tfrac1n H_n\to I(\theta_0)\succ0$ in $\Prob_{\theta_0}$-probability.
Suppose that after the whitening $z=H_n^{1/2}(\theta-\hat\theta_n)$ the rescaled posterior
$\bar\pi_n$ admits the representation
\[
  \frac{\dd\bar\pi_n}{\dd\gamma}(z)\ =\ \frac{e^{-r_n(z)}}{\int e^{-r_n}\dd\gamma},
  \qquad \gamma=N(0,\Id_d),
\]
with a global oscillation bound $\osc(r_n)=o_P(1)$. Then
\[
  \CP(\pi_n)\ =\ (1+o_P(1))\,\lmax(H_n^{-1})\ =\ \frac{1+o_P(1)}{n}\,\lmax\!\big(I(\theta_0)^{-1}\big),
\]
and, under the same hypothesis (which makes the perturbation Gaussian-comparable for LSI too),
\[
  \CLS(\pi_n),\ \CTCI(\pi_n)\ =\ \frac{1+o_P(1)}{n}\,\lmax\!\big(I(\theta_0)^{-1}\big).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put $\delta_n=\osc(r_n)$. The normalized density ratio with respect to $\gamma$ lies between
$e^{-\delta_n}$ and $e^{\delta_n}$. Holley--Stroock and the linear whitening map give the common
upper bound $e^{\delta_n}\lmax(H_n^{-1})$ for $\CP$ and $\CLS$; Otto--Villani gives it for
$\CTCI$. The same density-ratio sandwich implies
$\Cov_{\bar\pi_n}=I_d+o_P(1)$. Linear tests give the matching lower bound for $\CP$ and hence
$\CLS$; $T_2(C)$ implies the linear sub-Gaussian bound and therefore
$C\ge\lmax(\Cov)$, giving the matching lower bound for $\CTCI$. Finally
$H_n/n\to I(\theta_0)$ yields the displayed limits.
\end{proof}

\noindent Global $o_P(1)$ oscillation is a sufficient Gaussian-comparison hypothesis, not an
equivalent reformulation of an arbitrary tail/no-bottleneck condition. Replacing it by a weaker
condition for $\CP$ requires a separate spectral-stability theorem.

\subsection{A vanishing-leverage verification for logistic Poincar\'e constants}
\label{subsec:a2-leverage}

The A1 mode-leverage argument supplies one such theorem for the flagship bounded-Hessian model.
For a finite Gaussian-prior logistic posterior let $\hat\theta_n$ be the mode,
$\widehat H_n=\Hess V_n(\hat\theta_n)$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-max-leverage}
 \eta_n:=\max_{1\le i\le n}\sqrt{x_{n,i}^\top\widehat H_n^{-1}x_{n,i}}.
\end{equation}
Proposition~\ref{prop:a1-euclidean-harmonic} and
Corollary~\ref{cor:a1-leverage-asymptotic} give the deterministic implication
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-leverage-limit}
 \eta_n\to0
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \CP(\pi_n)=(1+o(1))\lmax(\widehat H_n^{-1})
 \qquad(d\text{ fixed}).
\end{equation}
The upper bound uses the factor-one inverse-curvature inequality and an explicit radial
mode-Hessian comparison. The same radial envelope gives an integrable exponential tail uniform
for all sufficiently small $\eta_n$, hence total-variation and second-moment convergence of the
$\widehat H_n$-whitened posterior to $N(0,I_d)$; the covariance linear test supplies the matching
lower bound. Thus, if additionally $\widehat H_n/n\to I(\theta_0)\succ0$ in probability,
\eqref{eq:a2-target} follows in probability.

This route does not assume finite global PL and does not require the globally vanishing
oscillation of Theorem~\ref{thm:a2-target}; ordinary logistic posteriors have neither property.
It instead assumes that no single observation dominates the mode-Hessian geometry. The conclusion
has passed independent agent review. Poisson weights satisfy the radial log-Hessian-Lipschitz
comparison, but their Hessian is unbounded, so the current noncompact factor-one domain proof does
not yet justify the exact Poisson coefficient.

\subsection{Subquadratic Gaussian-tail rigidity}
\label{subsec:a2-logistic-rigidity}

The LSI/transport conclusion above deliberately assumes a global Gaussian comparison. It fails
for the usual fixed Gaussian prior in binary logistic regression, even when the local BvM and
Poincar\'e conclusions are regular.

\begin{proposition}[Directional subquadratic Gaussian-tail rigidity]
\label{prop:a2-subquadratic-global}
Let $\Sigmaz\succ0$, let $m\in\R^d$, and let $L:\R^d\to\R$ be finite, convex, and $C^2$. Let
\[
 \pi(\dd\theta)\propto
 \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12(\theta-m)^\top\Sigmaz^{-1}(\theta-m)-L(\theta)\right]\dd\theta.
\]
Suppose that for some unit top eigenvector $u$ of $\Sigmaz$, with
$Y_t\sim N(m+t\Sigmaz u,\Sigmaz)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-subquadratic-direction}
  \E L(Y_t)=o(t^2)\qquad(t\to+\infty).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-subquadratic-rigidity}
  \CLS(\pi)=\CTCI(\pi)=\lmax(\Sigmaz).
\end{equation}
A sufficient condition is $L(\theta)\le a+b\norm\theta^p$ for some $a\in\R$, $b\ge0$, and
$0\le p<2$, or uniform
$L(\theta)=o(\norm\theta^2)$ at infinity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Completing the Gaussian square gives
\[
 \log\E_\pi e^{t u^\top\theta}
 =tu^\top m+\tfrac12t^2u^\top\Sigmaz u
  +\log\E e^{-L(Y_t)}-\log\E e^{-L(Y_0)}.
\]
Convexity supplies an affine lower support $L(y)\ge a_0+b_0^\top y$, so the third term is at most
$O(1+t)$. Jensen and \eqref{eq:a2-subquadratic-direction} bound it below by
$-\E L(Y_t)=o(t^2)$. Centering subtracts only
$t u^\top\E_\pi\theta$. Consequently
\[
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\frac2{t^2}
 \log\E_\pi e^{t u^\top(\theta-\E_\pi\theta)}
 =u^\top\Sigmaz u=\lmax(\Sigmaz).
\]
A $T_2(C)$ inequality forces $C\ge\lmax(\Sigmaz)$. Conversely,
$\Hess U\succeq\Sigmaz^{-1}$, so Bakry--\'Emery and Otto--Villani give
$\CTCI\le\CLS\le\lmax(\Sigmaz)$.
\end{proof}

For the transport lower bound alone, the full limit above is more than is needed: it is enough
that the corresponding one-directional centered log-MGF has quadratic limsup at least
$\lmax(\Sigmaz)$. Condition~\eqref{eq:a2-subquadratic-direction} is a transparent sufficient
contract that yields the full directional limit. Completing the square samples a fixed-covariance
Gaussian tube around the ray, so pointwise subquadratic growth on the ray alone is insufficient.
Centering is required by the standard sub-Gaussian formulation but changes only the linear term.
The proposition has passed independent agent review; a consolidated standalone proof records the
review boundary in \texttt{solutions/a2-subquadratic-tail-rigidity.tex}.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-logistic prior-tail rigidity]
\label{prop:a2-logistic-global}
Let $\Sigmaz\succ0$, let $m\in\R^d$, and let
\[
 \pi(\dd\theta)\propto
 \exp\!\left[-\tfrac12(\theta-m)^\top\Sigmaz^{-1}(\theta-m)-L(\theta)\right]\dd\theta,
\]
where $L$ is the standard binary-logistic negative log-likelihood of a finite data set
$(x_i,y_i)_{i=1}^n$, with $x_i\in\R^d$ and $y_i\in\{0,1\}$. Then
\[
  \CLS(\pi)=\CTCI(\pi)=\lmax(\Sigmaz).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For finite design, $0\le L(\theta)\le a+b\norm\theta$. Completing the Gaussian square and using
Gaussian moments gives \eqref{eq:a2-subquadratic-direction} for every direction. Apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:a2-subquadratic-global}.
\end{proof}

This exact identity is the finite-logistic corollary of the independently audited directional
theorem above.

With a fixed prior, therefore, $n\CLS(\pi_n)=n\CTCI(\pi_n)\to\infty$. The far-tail quadratic
coefficient is still the prior's, although the posterior bulk has Fisher covariance $O(n^{-1})$.
This gives a natural model in which the global entropy/transport constants are not Fisher-local
and shows that ``global log-concavity'' is not a sufficient condition for the LSI line of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:a2}.

There is also a sharp shrinking-prior curvature tradeoff. For logistic prior covariances $\Sigma_{0,n}$,
\eqref{eq:a2-subquadratic-rigidity} holds for every $n$. If the prior precision is negligible at
likelihood-curvature order,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-prior-negligible}
  \frac{\norm{\Sigma_{0,n}^{-1}}_{\rm op}}{n}\longrightarrow0,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a2-prior-tradeoff}
 n\CLS(\pi_n)=n\CTCI(\pi_n)
 =n\lmax(\Sigma_{0,n})
 \ge\frac{n}{\norm{\Sigma_{0,n}^{-1}}_{\rm op}}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
Conversely, $n\lmax(\Sigma_{0,n})\le C$ implies
$n^{-1}\Sigma_{0,n}^{-1}\succeq C^{-1}I$, so the prior contributes order-$n$ local curvature and
changes the ordinary Fisher target. An $n$-shrinking Gaussian prior therefore cannot both remain
negligible for the usual BvM information and restore global Fisher-scale LSI/$T_2$ in logistic
regression.
If the prior means $m_n$ move, full local prior negligibility additionally requires
$\norm{\Sigma_{0,n}^{-1}(\theta_0-m_n)}/\sqrt n\to0$ (or a model-adapted score condition);
\eqref{eq:a2-prior-negligible} is only the curvature component. The incompatibility remains valid
because curvature negligibility is necessary for the unmodified local information matrix.

\subsection{Sub-targets and what remains open}
\label{subsec:a2-open}

The single conjecture splits into four research questions of increasing difficulty, the first
of which is essentially a transfer exercise and the last of which is wide open.

\begin{question}[The weakest hypothesis for the Poincar\'e limit]
\label{q:a2-poincare}
Verify $\eta_n\to0$ under checkable random-design assumptions, identify the weakest replacement
for maximal leverage, and extend the result to
unbounded-Hessian GLMs such as Poisson. The deterministic low-temperature analogue
\eqref{eq:a2-cs-poincare} remains useful only when its finite global PL, growth, and
normalizability assumptions hold \cite{ChewiStromme2024Ballistic}; ordinary logistic regression
uses the different mode-leverage route.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[When is the LSI limit Fisher-local?]
\label{q:a2-lsi}
Characterize the models for which the limit is Fisher-local,
\[
  n\,\CLS(\pi_n)\to\lmax(I(\theta_0)^{-1}),
\]
versus those governed by a global PL or tail scale (Warning~\ref{warn:a2-lsi}). Ordinary
Gaussian-prior logistic regression already supplies an exact differing example through
Proposition~\ref{prop:a2-logistic-global}; Proposition~\ref{prop:a2-subquadratic-global} extends
that obstruction to a directional subquadratic tail class, and
\eqref{eq:a2-prior-tradeoff} rules out repairing logistic with a Gaussian prior that remains
asymptotically negligible. Find checkable genuinely quadratic-tail/global-PL classes for a
positive theorem, and formulate the localized alternative when it is not
\cite{BenNejma2026Lojasiewicz}.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Multimodal and weakly identified posteriors]
\label{q:a2-multimodal}
For mixtures, label switching, weak identification, and phase transitions the posterior is
multimodal and $\CP(\pi_n)$ can be \emph{exponentially} large in $n$ despite local Gaussianity
around each mode --- Eyring--Kramers / metastability behaviour rather than \eqref{eq:a2-target}.
Determine when the blow-up is physical and when it is a symmetry artifact removable by passing
to the quotient (the symmetry-quotient escape of Remark~\ref{rem:label-switching}, the
$e^{c\Delta^2}$ barrier of Theorem~\ref{thm:separated-mixture-lower}). This connects A2 to A5
(Section~\ref{sec:a5}, Conjecture~\ref{conj:a5-metastable}), where a two-well Gaussian orbit gives
an explicit calibration but the general quotient-BvM and metastability statements remain open.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Growing dimension $d=d_n$]
\label{q:a2-highdim}
Extend the sharp constants to the regime $d=d_n\to\infty$. MCMC mixing analyses already track
the joint $(d,n,\kappa)$ dependence using large-sample posterior Gaussianity
\cite{TangYang2024MALA}, and polynomial-time Langevin guarantees for high-dimensional
posteriors are known but deliberately avoid full asymptotic Gaussian approximation, relying
instead on local curvature and global log-concave comparison \cite{NicklWang2024Langevin};
exact Fisher-information asymptotics for $\CP,\CLS,\CTCI$ in growing dimension are far less
settled. Log-concave BvM theory \cite{Brunel2026LogConcaveBvM} and the finite-sample Laplace
bounds of \cite{KasprzakGiordanoBroderick2025} are the natural starting points.
\end{question}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the rest of the agenda and to numerics]
\label{rem:a2-relations}
A2 is the asymptotic counterpart of the finite-sample flagship A1
(Section~\ref{sec:a1}, Question~\ref{q:glm-data-informed}, Conjecture~\ref{conj:a1}): A1 seeks a
data-informed bound at fixed $n$ via Brascamp--Lieb (Theorem~\ref{thm:brascamp-lieb}) and
posterior concentration, while A2 seeks the exact $n\to\infty$ limit; a sharp A1 bound that is
tight in the bulk should reproduce \eqref{eq:a2-target} on letting $n\to\infty$, and consistency
between the two is a useful internal check. The three remaining siblings are the regimes in which
the clean limit \eqref{eq:a2-target} is qualified: transferring a TV-BvM limit to functional
inequalities requires global tail control that fails for the heavy-tailed posteriors of A3
(Section~\ref{sec:a3}, Warning~\ref{warn:a3-likelihood}), where weighted rather than classical
constants govern the behaviour; the asymptotic scale
$\tfrac1n\lmax(I(\theta_0)^{-1})$ is exactly the posterior-scale
target consumed by the variational-inference certificates of A4 (Section~\ref{sec:a4},
Question~\ref{q:a4-certificate}); and A5 asks whether the multimodal blow-up of
Question~\ref{q:a2-multimodal} disappears on the symmetry quotient
(Section~\ref{sec:a5}, Conjecture~\ref{conj:a5-metastable}). The Gaussian-orbit example verifies
that mechanism in one calibration, not for general mixture posteriors. Empirically, the lower bound
$\CP(\pi_n)\ge\lmax(\Cov_{\pi_n})$
(Remark~\ref{rem:rayleigh-lower-only}) and statistical estimators of the Poincar\'e constant
from samples \cite{PillaudVivienEtAl2020} give a numerical handle on the conjectured limit,
with the ratio of a curvature/perturbation upper bound to $\lmax(\Cov_{\pi_n})$ serving as the
sharpness diagnostic.
\end{remark}

\section{A5 --- Quotient constants and reparameterization constants}
\label{sec:a5}

This is the deep-dive companion to the A5 paragraph of the agenda (Section~\ref{sec:agenda})
and the formalization of Remarks~\ref{rem:label-switching} and \ref{rem:funnel}. The agenda
slogan, $\CP(\pi_{\Theta/S_K})\ll\CP(\pi_\Theta)$, is the right intuition but the wrong
universal statement. What is \emph{always} true is monotonicity,
\[
  \CP(\pi_{\Theta/G})\le\CP(\pi_\Theta),\qquad
  \CLS(\pi_{\Theta/G})\le\CLS(\pi_\Theta),\qquad
  \CTCI(\pi_{\Theta/G})\le\CTCI(\pi_\Theta),
\]
when the quotient carries the natural quotient metric and Dirichlet form. When the invariant gap
is positive, strict improvement requires the non-invariant restricted gap to be strictly smaller;
the mere presence of a tied non-invariant eigenfunction is insufficient. If the slower block is a
genuine permutation-invariant scientific alternative, the quotient does nothing. Making that
dichotomy precise --- and quantitative ---
is the content of A5. The parallel reparameterization question for hierarchical funnels turns
out to be a statement about which \emph{metric} the sampler sees, not about the abstract
posterior law.

\noindent\textbf{Reviewed status.}
Seven dossier-backed results have passed independent review: the invariant/non-invariant gap
ratio, its density-ratio stability, the exact Gaussian crossover, optimal scalar partial
non-centring, the exponential-tail consequence of Poincar\'e, Neal's funnel separation, and the
singular prior-only partial-funnel phase. The quotient-monotonicity theorem and Lipschitz transport
lemma are retained as elementary inline baselines but are not counted in that reviewed set. The
folding and $\mathbb Z_2$ bridge examples and the general metastability theorem remain explicitly
unreviewed drafts or conjectures.

\subsection{Setup: the symmetry quotient}
\label{subsec:a5-setup}

Let a finite group $G$ (for a $K$-component mixture, $G=S_K$ acting by permuting the component
blocks) act on the parameter space $\Theta$ by isometries, and let the posterior
$\pi(\dd\theta)\propto e^{-V(\theta)}\dd\theta$ be $G$-invariant ($V\circ g=V$ for all
$g\in G$). This posterior symmetry requires \emph{both} the prior and the likelihood to be
$G$-invariant; in a standard finite mixture it follows from an exchangeable prior together with
a likelihood invariant under component relabeling \cite{Celeux1998,JasraHolmesStephens2005}. Write
\[
  q:\Theta\to\bar\Theta:=\Theta/G,\qquad \bar\pi:=q_\#\pi,
\]
and equip the orbit space with the quotient distance and the quotient Dirichlet form obtained
by lifting,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-quotient-form}
  d_{\bar\Theta}([x],[y])=\min_{g\in G}d_\Theta(x,gy),
  \qquad
  \mathcal E_{\bar\pi}(\varphi):=\int_{\bar\Theta}\norm{\nabla_{\bar\Theta}\varphi}^2\dd\bar\pi
  =\int_{\Theta}\norm{\nabla(\varphi\circ q)}^2\dd\pi .
\end{equation}
Away from the collision strata $\{\theta_i=\theta_j\}$ this is the ordinary Riemannian quotient
energy; on the strata one uses weak/upper gradients or orbifold language
(Subsection~\ref{subsec:a5-singular}).

\subsection{The monotonicity theorem (the easy part)}
\label{subsec:a5-monotone}

\begin{theorem}[Quotient constants are never worse]
\label{thm:a5-monotone}
With the quotient structure \eqref{eq:a5-quotient-form} and $\pi$ $G$-invariant,
\[
  \CP(\bar\pi)\le\CP(\pi),\qquad
  \CLS(\bar\pi)\le\CLS(\pi),\qquad
  \CTCI(\bar\pi)\le\CTCI(\pi).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Poincar\'e and LSI by lifting test functions.} The pullback $\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ q$
is an isometry of $L^2(\bar\pi)$ onto the $G$-invariant subspace $L^2(\pi)^G$, and by
\eqref{eq:a5-quotient-form} it preserves the Dirichlet energy; it also preserves variance and
entropy, $\Var_{\bar\pi}(\varphi)=\Var_\pi(\varphi\circ q)$ and
$\Ent_{\bar\pi}(\varphi^2)=\Ent_\pi((\varphi\circ q)^2)$. Hence
\[
  \CP(\bar\pi)=\sup_{\varphi}\frac{\Var_{\bar\pi}(\varphi)}{\mathcal E_{\bar\pi}(\varphi)}
  =\sup_{f\in H^1(\pi)^G}\frac{\Var_\pi(f)}{\mathcal E_\pi(f)}
  \le\sup_{f\in H^1(\pi)}\frac{\Var_\pi(f)}{\mathcal E_\pi(f)}=\CP(\pi),
\]
the inequality being a restriction of the supremum to the invariant subspace; the LSI case is
identical with $\Ent$ in place of $\Var$.
\emph{Transport by lifting measures.} Given $\bar\nu\ll\bar\pi$, lift it via
$\dd\nu/\dd\pi=(\dd\bar\nu/\dd\bar\pi)\circ q$; then $\KL(\nu\|\pi)=\KL(\bar\nu\|\bar\pi)$,
while $q$ is $1$-Lipschitz so $W_{2,\bar\Theta}(\bar\nu,\bar\pi)\le W_{2,\Theta}(\nu,\pi)$.
Thus a $T_2$ inequality for $\pi$ (Definition~\ref{def:tci}) descends to $\bar\pi$ with no
worse constant \cite{GozlanRobertoSamson2013}.
\end{proof}

\noindent The theorem is elementary; the research content is \emph{when the inequality is
strict, and by how much}.

For the variational companion, Lemma~\ref{lem:a4-symmetrization} shows that averaging a competitor
over $G$ preserves invariant observables and decreases both reverse KL and squared $W_2$ distance
to an invariant target, provided the variational family admits the average.

\subsection{The exact dichotomy: invariant versus non-invariant slow modes}
\label{subsec:a5-spectral}

For Poincar\'e the gap is fully transparent spectrally. The generator
$L=\Delta-\nabla V\cdot\nabla$ commutes with the $G$-action because $V$ is invariant, so the
mean-zero space splits orthogonally into the invariant part and its complement,
\[
  L^2_0(\pi)=L^2_0(\pi)^G\ \oplus\ \big(L^2_0(\pi)^G\big)^\perp .
\]
Let $P_Gf=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}f\circ g$ be the $L^2(\pi)$ orthogonal projection onto the
invariant subspace. Define the two extended-valued restricted spectral gaps
\[
  \lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}=\inf_{\substack{f\in H^1(\pi)^G\\\Var_\pi(f)>0}}\frac{\mathcal E_\pi(f)}{\Var_\pi(f)},
  \qquad
  \lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}=\inf_{\substack{f\in H^1(\pi),\ P_Gf=0\\\Var_\pi(f)>0}}
  \frac{\mathcal E_\pi(f)}{\Var_\pi(f)} .
\]

\begin{proposition}[Quotient improvement ratio]
\label{prop:a5-ratio}
With the notation above,
\[
  \CP(\bar\pi)=\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}^{-1},
  \qquad
  \CP(\pi)=\big(\min\{\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}},\lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}\}\big)^{-1},
\]
with $1/0=+\infty$ and $1/(+\infty)=0$. If $0<\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}<\infty$, the ordinary improvement ratio is
\[
  \frac{\CP(\pi)}{\CP(\bar\pi)}=\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}}{\min\{\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}},\lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}\}} .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The $G$-invariance of $\pi$ and of its Dirichlet form makes $P_G$ self-adjoint for both the
$L^2$ and energy pairings. Thus every mean-zero $f$ decomposes as
$f=P_Gf+(I-P_G)f$ with
\[
 \Var_\pi(f)=\Var_\pi(P_Gf)+\Var_\pi((I-P_G)f),\qquad
 \mathcal E_\pi(f)=\mathcal E_\pi(P_Gf)+\mathcal E_\pi((I-P_G)f).
\]
Its Rayleigh quotient is therefore a variance-weighted average of the two block quotients. Taking
the infimum gives the minimum of the restricted gaps, and restricting to the invariant block gives
the quotient gap by the lifting identity \eqref{eq:a5-quotient-form}. Inverting yields the two
extended-valued constant identities; division is invoked only under the stated positivity and
finiteness condition.
\end{proof}

\noindent This proposition is independently agent-certified in
\path{solutions/prop-a5-ratio.tex}.

\noindent The identity gives the A5 dichotomy. When $0<\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}<\infty$,
strict improvement occurs exactly when
$\lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}<\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}$. If the two restricted gaps coincide, a
degenerate first eigenspace may contain both representation types but the quotient constant does
not improve. A label-switching bottleneck contributes non-invariant modes because they distinguish
one labeling from another; it produces a dramatic quotient gain only when their gap is strictly
below the invariant gap. A physical multimodal mode is invariant when it distinguishes two
genuinely different explanations \emph{after} relabeling; if that is the slower block, quotienting
does essentially nothing. This is the
precise form of ``symmetry-induced multimodality should not count against the constant;
physical multimodality should.''

\begin{proposition}[Invariant-block density-ratio stability]
\label{prop:a5-block-stability}
Let $\pi$ and $\widetilde\pi$ be $G$-invariant probability measures with the same underlying
metric carr\'e du champ $\Gamma(f)$, so that
$\mathcal E_\mu(f)=\int\Gamma(f)\,\dd\mu$ for either measure, and suppose
\[
 \operatorname*{osc}\log\frac{\dd\widetilde\pi}{\dd\pi}\le\varepsilon.
\]
Then, for $b\in\{\mathrm{inv},\mathrm{noninv}\}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-block-stability}
 e^{-\varepsilon}\lambda_b(\pi)
 \le\lambda_b(\widetilde\pi)
 \le e^{\varepsilon}\lambda_b(\pi),
\end{equation}
with the extended-value convention for an empty or zero-gap block. Consequently, if both gaps
are positive and finite, a strict block ordering is preserved whenever its logarithmic margin
exceeds $2\varepsilon$; for example
$\lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}(\pi)<e^{-2\varepsilon}\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}(\pi)$ implies
$\lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}(\widetilde\pi)<\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}(\widetilde\pi)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $h=\dd\widetilde\pi/\dd\pi$, $m=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}h$, and
$M=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}h$, so $M/m\le e^\varepsilon$. The variational formula
$\Var_\mu(f)=\inf_c\int(f-c)^2\,\dd\mu$ and direct energy comparison yield
\[
 m\Var_\pi(f)\le\Var_{\widetilde\pi}(f)
 \le M\Var_\pi(f),\qquad
 m\mathcal E_\pi(f)\le\mathcal E_{\widetilde\pi}(f)
 \le M\mathcal E_\pi(f).
\]
The pointwise group average $P_G$ is the orthogonal projection for both invariant measures, so
the invariant sector and its kernel are the same sets of functions. Comparing Rayleigh quotients
and taking the infimum separately in each fixed sector proves
$(m/M)\lambda_b(\pi)\le\lambda_b(\widetilde\pi)\le(M/m)\lambda_b(\pi)$, hence
\eqref{eq:a5-block-stability}.
\end{proof}

Stability of individual eigenvectors or spectral projectors needs isolated eigenvalues and
stronger operator control.

\begin{example}[Folding a separated double well (analytic proof draft)]
\label{ex:a5-folding}
Let $\mu_a=\tfrac12N(-a,\sigma^2)+\tfrac12N(a,\sigma^2)$ on $\R$, invariant under
$x\mapsto-x$, with quotient map $q(x)=\abs{x}$ and folded measure $\bar\mu_a=q_\#\mu_a$ on
$[0,\infty)$. A one-dimensional Hardy/Muckenhoupt comparison
(Theorem~\ref{thm:hardy-1d}) gives the logarithmic asymptotic
\[
 \log\!\frac{\CP(\mu_a)}{\sigma^2}
 =\frac{a^2}{2\sigma^2}+O\!\left(\log\!\left(1+\frac a\sigma\right)\right)
 \qquad (a/\sigma\to\infty).
\]
The error term deliberately retains the polynomial prefactor suppressed by a bare exponential
claim; identifying that prefactor exactly is a separate Hardy calculation. The quotient
satisfies $\bar\mu_a=\operatorname{Law}\abs{a+\sigma Z}$ for $Z\sim N(0,1)$, a $\sigma$-Lipschitz image of a
standard Gaussian. Hence
\[
 \CP(\bar\mu_a),\ \CLS(\bar\mu_a),\ \CTCI(\bar\mu_a)\le\sigma^2,
 \qquad \CP(\bar\mu_a)\longrightarrow\sigma^2
 \quad(a/\sigma\to\infty),
\]
where the limit follows from the linear test $\Var\abs{a+\sigma Z}\to\sigma^2$. Thus the raw to
quotient ratio has logarithmic rate $a^2/(2\sigma^2)$, up to polynomial factors --- the
finite-dimensional caricature of label switching. These analytic arguments remain a proof draft
pending repository certification.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[$\mathbb Z_2$ Gaussian-orbit statistical bridge (analytic proof draft)]
\label{ex:a5-z2-bridge}
For fixed $a,\sigma>0$ define the canonical posterior sequence
\[
 \mu_n=\tfrac12N(-a,\sigma^2/n)+\tfrac12N(a,\sigma^2/n),
 \qquad \bar\mu_n=\abs{\cdot}_\#\mu_n.
\]
Applying Example~\ref{ex:a5-folding} with within-well scale $\sigma/\sqrt n$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-z2-bridge}
 \frac1n\log\CP(\mu_n)\longrightarrow\frac{a^2}{2\sigma^2},
 \qquad n\CP(\bar\mu_n)\longrightarrow\sigma^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the raw logarithm has error $O(\log n)$, while
$\bar\mu_n=\operatorname{Law}\abs{a+\sigma Z/\sqrt n}$ has
$\CP(\bar\mu_n)\le\sigma^2/n$ and its quotient-coordinate variance supplies the matching limit.
The limiting risk $R(x)=(\abs{x}-a)^2/(2\sigma^2)$ has two wells and orbit-connectivity height
$a^2/(2\sigma^2)$.

This exact sequence also gives a strong, explicit bridge condition for a genuine symmetric
posterior $\widetilde\mu_n$. If
$\operatorname*{osc}\log(\dd\widetilde\mu_n/\dd\mu_n)=\varepsilon_n$, then
Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-block-stability} transfers the raw logarithmic rate when
$\varepsilon_n=o(n)$ and the quotient leading constant when $\varepsilon_n=o(1)$. Such a global
density-ratio hypothesis includes tail and bottleneck control and is much stronger than ordinary
total-variation BvM; \eqref{eq:a5-z2-bridge} is therefore a calibration and a conditional bridge,
not a general quotient-BvM theorem.
\end{example}

\subsection{Quotient Bernstein--von Mises: the metastable asymptotic}
\label{subsec:a5-metastable}

For a well-identified $G$-invariant $K$-component mixture, write the population risk as
$R(\theta)=-\E_{\theta_\star}\log p_\theta(Y)$ and let $R_\star=R(\theta_\star)$. Let
$\mathcal W=G\theta_\star$ be a free finite orbit of minima and define, for distinct
$w,w'\in\mathcal W$,
\[
 H(w,w'):=\inf_{\substack{\gamma\in C([0,1],\Theta)\\
                   \gamma(0)=w,\ \gamma(1)=w'}}
 \max_{t\in[0,1]}\{R(\gamma(t))-R_\star\}.
\]
The finite-network barrier entering the conjectural global spectral-gap exponent is the height at
which the entire orbit network becomes connected:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-barrier}
 \Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}
 :=\inf\{h:\ (\mathcal W,\{\{w,w'\}:H(w,w')\le h\})\text{ is connected}\}
 =\max_{\varnothing\ne A\subsetneq\mathcal W}
   \min_{\substack{w\in A\\w'\in\mathcal W\setminus A}}H(w,w').
\end{equation}
The equality is the finite-graph bottleneck identity: every connected threshold graph crosses
every nontrivial cut, while below the connectivity threshold one of its connected components
defines an un-crossed cut. The simpler minimum pairwise height is correct only when the graph of
minimum-height orbit moves is already connected (equivalently, in a group orbit, when those moves
generate a connected Cayley graph). This is automatic for a two-well $\mathbb Z_2$ orbit but
false in general.

\noindent The finite-graph worst-cut/connectivity identity above was independently checked. This
does not certify the posterior metastability limit, which remains Conjecture~\ref{conj:a5-metastable}.

A quantitative label-switching regime asks for $\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}>0$ and, for the collision stratum
$\mathcal C=\{\theta:\text{the $G$-action is not free at $\theta$}\}$, constants
$\delta,c_{\rm col}>0$ such that
\[
 \inf_{d(\theta,\mathcal C)\le\delta}\{R(\theta)-R_\star\}\ge c_{\rm col}.
\]
It also needs coercive population tails and a uniform empirical landscape approximation on the
relevant compact sets, strong enough that the empirical communication height
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn},n}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}+o_{\mathbb P_{\theta_\star}}(1)$ and the
corresponding capacity estimates hold.
Only under such quantitative hypotheses does energy-landscape theory
\cite{MenzSchlichting2014} predict the raw exponential rate while the quotient recovers the local
Laplace / Bernstein--von Mises scale of A2 (Section~\ref{sec:a2}).

\begin{conjecture}[Raw-versus-quotient metastability]
\label{conj:a5-metastable}
For fixed $K$, suppose both prior and likelihood are $G$-invariant, the population risk has a
single physical minimum modulo $G$, its orbit is free with uniformly nondegenerate transverse
Hessians, and the connectivity barrier $\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}$ in \eqref{eq:a5-barrier} is
positive. Assume quantitatively the
collision gap above, posterior tail coercivity outside a fixed compact set, a prior whose
log-density is $o(n)$ on the relevant landscape, uniform empirical risk/derivative control giving
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn},n}\to\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}$ in
$\mathbb P_{\theta_\star}$-probability, and matching upper and lower
capacity bounds with subexponential errors. Then
\[
  \frac1n\log\CP(\pi_\Theta)\ \xrightarrow{\mathbb P_{\theta_\star}}\ \Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}},
  \qquad
  \CP(\pi_{\Theta/S_K})\ =\ \frac{1+o_P(1)}{n}\,\lmax\!\big(I(\theta_\star)^{-1}\big),
\]
where the quotient expansion is likewise in $\mathbb P_{\theta_\star}$-probability. Where the
landscape decomposition and tail hypotheses extend, analogous statements may be sought for
$\CLS$ and $\CTCI$; polynomial/Eyring--Kramers prefactors are a separate second target.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent The quotient line is exactly the A2 target \eqref{eq:a2-target}: the conjecture says
that, under its quantitative landscape and tail hypotheses, removing the symmetry artifact leaves
the BvM asymptotic sought in A2 (Conjecture~\ref{conj:a2}). The driver of the raw blow-up is the connectivity barrier
$\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}$ and the representation
type of the slow mode, \emph{not} the count $K!$ --- $K!$ creates redundant wells, but the bad
constant comes from the exponentially suppressed transitions between them. The benign regime
where even the raw mixture keeps a good constant is the bounded-overlap $\chi^2$ case of
Corollary~\ref{cor:ccnw-mixture-lsi}.

\subsection{The quotient can be singular}
\label{subsec:a5-singular}

$\Theta/S_K$ is in general an orbifold / stratified space, not a smooth manifold; the singular
strata are the collisions $\theta_i=\theta_j$. If the posterior puts negligible mass near them,
the quotient behaves like a fundamental chamber with reflecting walls and the analysis of
Subsections~\ref{subsec:a5-monotone}--\ref{subsec:a5-metastable} applies. If it puts
substantial mass near collisions --- weak data, overfitting, empty or merging components ---
the quotient geometry is genuinely singular and $\CP,\CLS,\CTCI$ may remain bad even after
quotienting. This is also why a hard ordering constraint $\mu_1<\cdots<\mu_K$ is not
automatically benign: it selects a fundamental domain but may cut through high-posterior regions
or create awkward boundary behaviour \cite{Stephens2000,JasraHolmesStephens2005}. The clean
object is the quotient metric space, not an arbitrary ordering rule; recent computational work
realizes quotient inference directly via optimal transport on the orbit space
\cite{MonteillerEtAl2019} and via fundamental-domain / symmetrized flows
\cite{FoldedTransport2026}, and symmetry-aware mixing analyses obtain polynomial-time guarantees
where local chains would tunnel exponentially \cite{MouEtAl2019}.

\subsection{Funnels: reparameterization is a statement about the metric}
\label{subsec:a5-funnel}

The constants $\CP,\CLS,\CTCI$ are \emph{not} coordinate-invariant unless the metric is
transported with the coordinates. Centered and non-centered parameterizations describe the same
abstract law but a different Euclidean Dirichlet form,
\[
  \int\norm{\nabla_{\theta,\tau}f}^2\dd\pi
  \quad\text{versus}\quad
  \int\norm{\nabla_{z,\tau}f}^2\dd\pi_{\mathrm{ncp}},
\]
so ``non-centering improves the constant'' means: it improves the constant for the Euclidean
geometry that standard Langevin/HMC actually sees. With the exact pullback metric the constants
would be invariant. The quantitative tool is the Lipschitz transport rule.

\begin{lemma}[Lipschitz transport of all three constants]
\label{lem:a5-lipschitz}
If $T_\#\nu=\mu$ with $T$ globally $L$-Lipschitz, then
$\CP(\mu)\le L^2\CP(\nu)$, $\CLS(\mu)\le L^2\CLS(\nu)$, and $\CTCI(\mu)\le L^2\CTCI(\nu)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Test with $f\mapsto f\circ T$ and use $\norm{\nabla(f\circ T)}\le L\,(\norm{\nabla f}\circ T)$
together with $\Var_\nu(f\circ T)=\Var_\mu(f)$ (resp.\ $\Ent$); for $T_2$, push couplings
forward through $T$. More precisely, for $q\ll\mu$ lift it by
$\dd\tilde q/\dd\nu=(\dd q/\dd\mu)\circ T$; then $T_\#\tilde q=q$ and
$\KL(\tilde q\|\nu)=\KL(q\|\mu)$, while pushing a coupling forward contracts $\Wtwo$ by $L$.
\end{proof}

\noindent A funnel is exactly where the centered$\leftrightarrow$non-centered map fails to be
globally bi-Lipschitz. For Neal's map neither direction is globally Lipschitz, so the lemma gives
no global comparison; the constants can genuinely differ.

Raw constants cannot by themselves define an ``optimal'' parameterization: a scalar dilation by
$a$ multiplies each of $\CP,\CLS,\CTCI$ by $a^2$. A comparison must fix a physical metric or use
a scale-free objective. For $\dd\mu\propto e^{-V}\dd x$ with
$L_V=\sup_x\norm{\Hess V(x)}_{\rm op}<\infty$, one such calibration is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-condition}
  \kappa_P(\mu):=L_V\CP(\mu),
\end{equation}
which is dilation-invariant and equals the covariance condition number for a Gaussian.

\begin{example}[Exact Gaussian centred/non-centred crossover]
\label{ex:a5-gaussian-crossover}
Let $u,z\sim N(0,1)$ independently, $\theta=u+z$, and
$y\mid\theta\sim N(\theta,r^{-1})$ for likelihood precision $r>0$, with $r=0$ denoting no
observation. In centred coordinates $(u,\theta)$ and non-centred
coordinates $(u,z)$ the posterior precision matrices are
\[
 H_{\rm c}=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&1+r\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_{\rm nc}=\begin{pmatrix}1+r&r\\r&1+r\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their eigenvalues show exactly that non-centring has smaller $\CP$ and $\kappa_P$ for $0\le r<1$,
centring has smaller values for $r>1$, and the two tie at $r=1$. This normalized finite-data
model is the calibration anchor for partial non-centring. The calculation is independently
agent-certified in \path{solutions/ex-a5-gaussian-crossover.tex}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal scalar partial non-centring]
\label{prop:a5-partial-gaussian}
Let
\[
 u\sim N(0,A),\qquad \theta\mid u\sim N(u,B),\qquad
 L_y(\theta)\propto e^{-r(\theta-y)^2/2},
 \qquad A,B>0,\ r\ge0,
\]
and use the partial coordinate $z_\alpha=\theta-\alpha u$, $0\le\alpha\le1$. Its posterior
precision in $(u,z_\alpha)$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-partial-precision}
 H_\alpha=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A^{-1}+(1-\alpha)^2B^{-1}+r\alpha^2&-(1-\alpha)B^{-1}+r\alpha\\
 -(1-\alpha)B^{-1}+r\alpha&B^{-1}+r
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Both the Gaussian Poincar\'e constant and the condition number $\kappa_P$ are uniquely minimized
at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:a5-partial-optimum}
 \boxed{\alpha_\star=\frac{1}{1+rB}.}
\end{equation}
Thus the optimum moves continuously from non-centred at $r=0$ to centred as $rB\to\infty$;
$A$ affects the attained constant but not the optimal interpolation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The shear $(u,\theta)\mapsto(u,z_\alpha)$ has determinant one, hence
$\det H_\alpha$ is independent of $\alpha$. For a two-dimensional positive-definite matrix at
fixed determinant, maximizing its smaller eigenvalue and minimizing its eigenvalue condition
number are both equivalent to minimizing its trace. From \eqref{eq:a5-partial-precision},
\[
 \operatorname{tr}H_\alpha
 =\text{constant}+(B^{-1}+r)\alpha^2-2B^{-1}\alpha,
\]
whose unique minimizer on $[0,1]$ is \eqref{eq:a5-partial-optimum}. Since
$\CP=1/\lambda_{\min}(H_\alpha)$ and
$\kappa_P=\lambda_{\max}(H_\alpha)/\lambda_{\min}(H_\alpha)$, both objectives have that same
minimizer. The observation $y$ changes only the posterior mean.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Poincar\'e forces a Lipschitz exponential moment]
\label{lem:a5-pi-exp-tail}
If a probability law satisfies a Poincar\'e inequality with finite constant $C$, then every
real $1$-Lipschitz $f\in L^2$ has
$\E e^{c|f-\operatorname{med}f|}<\infty$ for
$0<c<(\log2)/(2\sqrt C)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $m$ be a median, set $a=2\sqrt C$, and, for $t\ge0$, apply Poincar\'e to the ramp
$g=\min\{(f-m-t)_+,a\}$. The independent-copy variance identity and
$\P(f\le m+t)\ge1/2$ give
\[
 \tfrac12a^2\P(f\ge m+t+a)\le\Var(g).
\]
The chain rule gives
$\int\norm{\nabla g}^2\dd\mu\le\P(m+t<f<m+t+a)$. Consequently
\[
 \P(f\ge m+t+a)\le\frac{2C}{a^2}\P(f>m+t)=\tfrac12\P(f>m+t).
\]
Iteration gives a geometric upper tail on intervals of length $a$. Applying the argument to
$-f$ gives the lower tail, and tail integration proves the exponential moment.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Neal's funnel: $\infty$ versus Gaussian scale]
\label{ex:a5-neal}
For $s>0$, $u\sim N(0,s^2)$, $z_j\sim N(0,1)$ independent, and $\theta_j=e^u z_j$
\cite{Neal2003Slice}, the non-centered coordinates $(u,z)$ have product-Gaussian law, so by
tensorization (Theorem~\ref{thm:tensorization}) and Family~\ref{fam:gaussian-covariance}
\[
  \CP^{\mathrm{ncp}}=\CLS^{\mathrm{ncp}}=\CTCI^{\mathrm{ncp}}=\max\{s^2,1\}.
\]
In centered coordinates $(u,\theta)$ the map $T(u,z)=(u,e^uz)$ has unbounded derivative, and the
marginal $\theta_j=e^uz_j$ lies in $L^2$ with $\E\theta_j^2=e^{2s^2}$ but has lognormal-type
tails with $\E\,e^{a\abs{\theta_j}}=\infty$ for
every $a>0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-pi-exp-tail} would force exponential integrability of this
Lipschitz coordinate about a median $m$ under a finite Poincar\'e inequality; but
$e^{c\abs{\theta_j}}\le e^{c\abs m}e^{c\abs{\theta_j-m}}$ would then make the uncentred moment
finite. Hence
the centered funnel satisfies \emph{no} finite Euclidean Poincar\'e inequality, hence no
Euclidean LSI or $T_2$:
\[
  \CP^{\mathrm{cen}}=\infty,
  \qquad
  \CP^{\mathrm{ncp}}=\max\{s^2,1\}.
\]
The independently agent-certified argument is recorded in
\path{solutions/ex-a5-neal.tex}. It formalizes why non-centering removes the canonical funnel
in the weak-data regime \cite{BetancourtGirolami2015}.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[The prior-only partial-funnel phase is singular]
\label{prop:a5-partial-funnel}
In the preceding Neal funnel with $s>0$, put
$z_\alpha=e^{-\alpha u}\theta=e^{(1-\alpha)u}z$ and give $(u,z_\alpha)$ its Euclidean metric.
For every $0\le\alpha<1$,
\[
 \CP\big(\operatorname{Law}(u,z_\alpha)\big)=+\infty,
\]
whereas at $\alpha=1$ the law is the product Gaussian and
$\CP=\max\{s^2,1\}$. Thus the global Euclidean $\CP$ objective has no smooth interior partial-
noncentring phase in the prior-only canonical funnel: its only finite point on $[0,1]$ is full
non-centring.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $\beta=1-\alpha>0$, the coordinate $z_\alpha=e^{\beta u}z$ lies in $L^2$, with
$\E z_\alpha^2=e^{2\beta^2s^2}$, but has no finite exponential moment of
$\abs{z_\alpha}$: conditioning on any fixed event $\{\abs z\ge1\}$ leaves an exponent
$ce^{\beta u}-u^2/(2s^2)\to+\infty$ in the Gaussian $u$ integral. The coordinate map
$(u,z_\alpha)\mapsto z_\alpha$ is
one-Lipschitz. As above, an exponential moment about a median would imply an uncentred one by
the triangle inequality, so Lemma~\ref{lem:a5-pi-exp-tail} contradicts a finite Poincar\'e
inequality.
At $\alpha=1$, $z_\alpha=z$ and tensorization
gives the displayed finite constant.
\end{proof}

\noindent The independently agent-certified argument is recorded in
\path{solutions/prop-a5-partial-funnel.tex}.

This phase statement is restricted to the prior-only/weak-data law. A likelihood depending on
$\theta=e^{\alpha u}z_\alpha$ may suppress the offending tails, but finite $\CP$ then requires a
model-specific integrability or Lyapunov argument; a high-probability local bi-Lipschitz bound is
not enough. Moreover, for $\alpha<1$ the partial-coordinate potential has unbounded Hessian, so
the global smoothness objective $L_V\CP$ is not a finite substitute in this example.

\begin{warning}[Non-centered is not always better]
\label{warn:a5-ncp}
With strong likelihood information the centered parameterization can win, because the data
identify $\theta_j$ directly while the non-centered likelihood $L(e^uz)$ couples $u$ and $z$.
This is the classical centered/non-centered trade-off
\cite{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007}; the practical resolution is often \emph{partial}
non-centering or the ancillarity--sufficiency interweaving strategy \cite{YuMeng2011}, neither
``centered'' nor ``non-centered'' alone.
\end{warning}

\subsection{What is genuinely open}
\label{subsec:a5-open}

The seven independently reviewed quotient and funnel results above settle the exact finite-model
calibrations and the relevant structural obstructions. The following are the remaining research
targets, in increasing difficulty; the elementary monotonicity theorem alone does not settle any
strict improvement or posterior asymptotic.

\begin{question}[Quotient Bernstein--von Mises constants]
\label{q:a5-qbvm}
Prove the quotient line of Conjecture~\ref{conj:a5-metastable}: under identifiability modulo
$S_K$, separation from collision strata, and no physical multimodality,
$\CP(\pi_{\Theta/S_K})=\tfrac{1+o_P(1)}{n}\lmax(I(\theta_\star)^{-1})$. This is the A2 program
(Section~\ref{sec:a2}) executed on the orbit space, and is the cleanest bridge between A2 and A5.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Raw-versus-quotient metastability with explicit barrier]
\label{q:a5-metastable}
First prove the logarithmic barrier law
$n^{-1}\log\CP(\pi_\Theta)\to\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}$ in
$\mathbb P_{\theta_\star}$-probability, with $\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}$ the population
worst-cut/connectivity height \eqref{eq:a5-barrier}. Verify quantitatively the collision gap,
empirical-barrier convergence, coercive tails, and two-sided capacity estimates rather than
assuming that posterior mass is merely ``away'' from the collision set. Only then seek the
Eyring--Kramers polynomial prefactor. Together with Question~\ref{q:a5-qbvm} this makes
Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-ratio} quantitative without hiding assumptions in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Detecting the representation type of the slow mode]
\label{q:a5-detect}
When the first eigenspace is degenerate, assigning a representation type to one fitted
eigenfunction is unstable. Instead split a group-stable Ritz basis with
$P_Gf=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}f\circ g$, solve the invariant and orthogonal generalized
eigenproblems, and attach residual/two-sided error bounds. The test must decide whether
$\lambda_{\mathrm{noninv}}<\lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}$, including equality, from posterior samples
\cite{PillaudVivienEtAl2020}.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Quotient LSI and $T_2$ on stratified spaces]
\label{q:a5-stratified}
Develop $\CLS$ and $\CTCI$ theory on stratified Bayesian quotient spaces. Poincar\'e is
spectral and clean (Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-ratio}); LSI and $T_2$ are more delicate near
collision strata and for overfitted mixtures, where the relevant mixture FI tools
\cite{ChafaiMalrieu2010,Schlichting2019,ChenChewiNilesWeed2021} must be adapted to the orbifold
geometry of Subsection~\ref{subsec:a5-singular}.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Finite-sample centered/non-centered comparison]
\label{q:a5-reparam}
For hierarchical models compare parameterizations only after fixing a common physical
normalization, or through a scale-invariant objective such as \eqref{eq:a5-condition} or
bi-Lipschitz distortion. Use Example~\ref{ex:a5-gaussian-crossover} as the exact calibration and
Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-partial-gaussian} as the solved scalar partial-noncentring problem before
treating nonlinear funnels. Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-partial-funnel} shows that the prior-only
nonlinear global-$\CP$ phase can be singular rather than a smooth interpolation.
Where a bi-Lipschitz comparison is available, prove two-sided constants
\[
  \CP^{\mathrm{cen}}\le A(n,\text{data},p(\tau))\,\CP^{\mathrm{ncp}},
  \qquad
  \CP^{\mathrm{ncp}}\le B(n,\text{data},p(\tau))\,\CP^{\mathrm{cen}},
\]
with $A,B$ exposing the weak-data and strong-data regimes (Warning~\ref{warn:a5-ncp}), going
beyond the algorithmic/qualitative theory of \cite{PapaspiliopoulosRobertsSkold2007,YuMeng2011}.
Separately, \emph{separate tail failure from funnel geometry}: a half-Cauchy $p(\tau)$ can
defeat Poincar\'e even non-centered (a Tier-4 heavy-tail obstruction, Section~\ref{sec:tier4}),
whereas a $\log\tau$ with Gaussian tails makes non-centering fix the canonical funnel
(Example~\ref{ex:a5-neal}); Lyapunov-function criteria
\cite{BakryCattiauxGuillin2008,CattiauxGuillinWangWu2009} are the tool for existence beyond
uniform convexity.
\end{question}

\begin{remark}[Relation to the rest of the agenda]
\label{rem:a5-relations}
A5 is the symmetry/geometry face of the agenda, and it supplies the escape hatch the other targets
invoke. Its monotonicity-and-metastability dichotomy (Theorem~\ref{thm:a5-monotone},
Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-ratio}) is what lets A2 (Section~\ref{sec:a2},
Question~\ref{q:a2-multimodal}) distinguish a genuine $e^{n\Gamma_{\mathrm{conn}}}$ blow-up from a removable
symmetry artifact, and on the quotient the A2 Bernstein--von Mises asymptotic \eqref{eq:a2-target}
is exactly what survives (Conjecture~\ref{conj:a5-metastable}); the family-dependent multimodal
lower bounds and quotient certificate of A4 (Section~\ref{sec:a4}, Remark~\ref{rem:a4-quotient})
are the variational consumers of the same dichotomy. The funnel half is the reparameterization
counterpart of the flat-direction obstruction of the flagship A1 (Section~\ref{sec:a1},
Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-prior-nonintegrable}); and the half-Cauchy tail that defeats Poincar\'e
even after non-centering (Question~\ref{q:a5-reparam}) is a heavy-tailed A3 obstruction
(Section~\ref{sec:a3}, Warning~\ref{warn:a3-likelihood}), distinct from the funnel geometry itself.
\end{remark}

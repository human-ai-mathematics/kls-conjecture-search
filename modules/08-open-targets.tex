\section{Open targets and research agenda}
\label{sec:agenda}

The live frontier of Part I after the first proof cycles. The A-series now contains twenty-one reviewed sharp
model results, local and blockwise theorems, and decisive obstructions, but general
posterior-specific constants remain scarce. The agenda below records both the promoted results and
the smaller residual set of open problems for
$\pi(\theta\mid y)\propto e^{-U_n(\theta)}$.

\paragraph{A1 --- data-informed GLM constants (Question~\ref{q:glm-data-informed}).}
The flagship open problem. The certified bound $\CLS\le\lmax(\Sigmaz)$ ignores the data;
near the posterior bulk $\Hess U\approx\Sigmaz^{-1}+X^\top W(\hat\theta)X$ is far larger.
The target is a bound of the shape
\[
  \CP(\pi)\ \lesssim\ \lmax\!\Big[\big(\Sigmaz^{-1}+X^\top\bar W X\big)^{-1}\Big]
  \ +\ \text{tail correction},
\]
interpolating prior- and likelihood-dominated regimes. For saturating links such as logistic
regression, the tension is intrinsic: likelihood curvature is strong in the bulk but vanishes in
remote predictor directions (separable data, Proposition~\ref{prop:flat-prior-nonintegrable}), so
no positive global likelihood-curvature floor exists. Gaussian-linear and other uniformly curved
models are calibration exceptions.
Candidate tools: Brascamp--Lieb (Theorem~\ref{thm:brascamp-lieb}), Lyapunov drift,
localization, and posterior-concentration control
\cite{BakryCattiauxGuillin2008,CattiauxGuillinWangWu2009,cattiaux2018poincare}.
The independently agent-certified results sharpen this programme in the bounded-Hessian class:
Proposition~\ref{prop:a1-euclidean-harmonic} gives factor one for the average inverse minimum
curvature, while Proposition~\ref{prop:a1-mode-leverage} turns the Hessian at the mode and the
maximal row leverage into explicit radial bulk and tail integrals.  Vanishing maximal leverage
then gives the exact fixed-dimensional Fisher coefficient
(Corollary~\ref{cor:a1-leverage-asymptotic}).  The frontier is unbounded-Hessian
Poisson/nonsmooth extensions, independent review of the analytic universal bulk--tail comparison, and less
conservative high-leverage spectral certificates.

\paragraph{A2 --- asymptotic constants via Bernstein--von Mises.}
For regular parametric models the posterior is asymptotically Gaussian,
$\pi_n\approx N(\hat\theta_n,(nI(\theta_0))^{-1})$, suggesting
\[
  \CP(\pi_n)\ =\ \tfrac{1+o(1)}{n}\,\lmax\!\big(I(\theta_0)^{-1}\big).
\]
For $\CLS$ and $\CTCI$, the same limit requires a genuinely global tail comparison (or a
shrinking prior): ordinary fixed-prior logistic posteriors retain the prior-scale global constants.
A sharp Poincar\'e theorem, together with a classification of those stronger entropy/transport
hypotheses, would turn classical asymptotic normality into asymptotic sampling/concentration
constants.
For finite logistic posteriors, the A1 leverage route now proves the
$C_P$ limit without a global density-ratio comparison. In the opposite direction,
Proposition~\ref{prop:a2-subquadratic-global} shows that any convex subquadratic Gaussian-prior
perturbation retains the exact prior-scale global $C_{\rm LS}=C_{\rm TCI}$; shrinking the prior
enough to change that scale also changes the local information at order $n$.

\paragraph{A3 --- heavy-tailed posteriors beyond classical LSI (Section~\ref{sec:tier4}).}
Make Family~\ref{fam:heavy-tail-classification} quantitative: replace the failed LSI/$T_2$ by
modified or weighted inequalities, with tail-index-dependent Poincar\'e constants for
Student-$t$, horseshoe, and scale-mixture hierarchical priors, certified by the $1$D Hardy
theory (Theorem~\ref{thm:hardy-1d}) and tensorization.
The one-dimensional horseshoe calibration is now solved: a positive rational $E_1$ remainder,
an intrinsic half-line estimate, and a parity/trace argument give
$C_{\rm HS}=4$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:a3-horseshoe}).  The certified fixed-Cauchy-marginal
counterexample proves that arbitrary dependence is not controlled by marginal Hardy constants.
The quantitative replacement is the block-resampling gap of
Theorem~\ref{thm:a3-block-gibbs}, equal to one minus HGR maximal correlation for two blocks; the
Bayesian frontier is to bound that coupling factor for explicit global--local posteriors.

\paragraph{A4 --- transport constants for variational inference.}
Variational Bayes minimizes $\KL(q\|\pi)$ over a family $\mathcal Q$, yet the quantity of interest
is geometric: $\Wtwo(q,\pi)$, or the posterior-mean error $\norm{\E_q\theta-\E_\pi\theta}$. Since
$\Wtwo^2(q,\pi)\le2\CTCI\,\KL(q\|\pi)$ by $T_2$ (Definition~\ref{def:tci}), $\CTCI$ certifies
exactly when KL is a reliable proxy for geometric closeness --- small for Gaussian-prior log-concave
posteriors (Section~\ref{sec:tier3}), absent for heavy tails (Section~\ref{sec:tier4}), enormous for
multimodal targets (Section~\ref{sec:tier5}). Turning this into certified VB error bars has three
fronts: (i) the \emph{family-restricted} constant, the smallest $C$ with
$\Wtwo^2(q,\pi)\le2C\,\KL(q\|\pi)$ for all $q\in\mathcal Q$, which may be far below the
global $\CTCI$ but is not automatically finite (target: a sharp $C$
for the Section~\ref{sec:tier3} GLM posteriors); (ii) the linear posterior-mean constant bounding
$\norm{\E_q\theta-\E_\pi\theta}$ directly --- the number a VB user reports and a weaker object
than a genuine $T_1$ inequality for all Lipschitz observables; (iii) quantitative Tier-4/5 verdicts --- a weighted transport
inequality replacing the failed $T_2$ for heavy tails (the $\Wtwo$ analogue of A3), and
family-admissible multimodal lower witnesses. The Gaussian mode-collapse witness currently proves
quadratic separation growth; the component-reweighting crossover and any barrier-scale asymptotic
remain open. A symmetry quotient removes only the non-invariant part of this obstruction.
Locally there are three different questions, now separated by agent-certified A4 theorems: a
well-specified raw constant, a misspecified raw constant with nonzero approximation baseline, and
an optimizer-centred excess-KL constant. Only the first and third have the Wasserstein/Fisher
generalized-eigenvalue limit; Example~\ref{ex:a4-gaussian-local} calibrates all three exactly.
The exact global and localized posterior-mean duals and the variational symmetrization lemma are
also independently reviewed.
The remaining posterior theorem must control a finite optimizer sublevel by explicit bulk and
tail remainders rather than relying on the tangent expansion alone.

\paragraph{A5 --- mixture and quotient constants.}
Formalize Remark~\ref{rem:label-switching}: characterize strict improvement,
\[
  \CP(\pi_{\Theta/S_K})<\CP(\pi_\Theta),
\]
for mixture posteriors. Strict improvement occurs only
when the slowest spectral sector is non-invariant; invariant physical bottlenecks can survive the
quotient. Also quantify funnel reparameterizations under a fixed normalization
(Remark~\ref{rem:funnel}).
For a finite orbit, the candidate metastable exponent must use the worst-cut/connectivity height
$\Gamma_{\rm conn}$, not the easiest pairwise move.  A two-well Gaussian orbit gives an exact
analytic raw/quotient calibration still awaiting a standalone Hardy review
(Example~\ref{ex:a5-z2-bridge}), while the scalar Gaussian hierarchy has the certified exact
partial-noncentring optimum $\alpha_*=1/(1+rB)$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:a5-partial-gaussian}).  The prior-only Neal funnel has the complementary
singular verdict: every $\alpha<1$ still has infinite Euclidean $C_P$.  The next step is a smooth
statistical multiwell theorem and likelihood-dependent partial-noncentring theory.

\paragraph{A1-bis --- structured-posterior KLS.}
\begin{conjecture}[Structured-posterior KLS]
\label{conj:a1-bis}
For a GLM posterior
with $\ell$ convex and local Hessian $H(\theta)=\Sigmaz^{-1}+\sum_i\ell''(y_i,x_i^\top\theta)x_ix_i^\top$,
prove, for a useful explicitly specified class, a Bayesian-statistical analogue of KLS of the form
\[
  \CP(\pi)\ \le\ A\,\E_\pi\,\lmax\!\big(H(\theta)^{-1}\big)+B\cdot(\text{tail correction}),
  \qquad\text{or even}\qquad
  \CP(\pi)\ \le\ K\,\lmax(\Cov_\pi)
\]
for a useful class of GLM posteriors, with $K$ universal or mildly geometry-dependent. The
finite-sum structure $U_n(\theta)=\sum_i\ell_i(x_i^\top\theta)+r(\theta)$ is exactly the extra
structure that the general convex-geometry literature does not exploit --- and exploiting it
is what makes the structured problem more tractable than the general one.
\end{conjecture}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Bridge to Part III.} The bound $\CP\le K\,\lmax(\Cov_\pi)$ for \emph{every}
isotropic log-concave measure, with $K$ universal and dimension-free, is no longer a Bayesian
question --- it is the Kannan--Lov\'asz--Simonovits conjecture. That Tier-$\infty$ boundary,
and a conditional program attacking it via stochastic localization, is the subject of
Part~III.

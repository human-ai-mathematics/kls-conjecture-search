\section{KLS: target, 2026 frontier, and strategy map}
\label{sec:kls-strategy-map}

Let $\mu$ be an isotropic log-concave probability measure on $\R^n$. The Poincar\'e form of the
Kannan--Lov\'asz--Simonovits conjecture is the following.

\begin{conjecture}[KLS]
\label{conj:kls}
There is a universal constant $C<\infty$ such that, for every dimension, every isotropic
log-concave $\mu$, and every locally Lipschitz $f$,
\[
  \Var_\mu(f)\le C\int_{\R^n}|\nabla f|^2\dd\mu.
\]
Equivalently up to universal constants, the Cheeger constants of all such measures are bounded
below uniformly in the dimension.
\end{conjecture}

Write
\begin{equation}
  \hstar_n=\inf\bigl\{h_\nu:\ \nu\ \text{isotropic log-concave on }\R^n\bigr\}.
  \label{eq:hstar-def}
\end{equation}
Thus Conjecture~\ref{conj:kls} is equivalently $\inf_n\hstar_n>0$, up to the universal
Cheeger--Poincar\'e comparison below.

With the normalization of Section~\ref{sec:introduction}, Cheeger's inequality gives
$\CP(\mu)\le4h_\mu^{-2}$; the reverse comparison for log-concave measures is also up to a
universal constant. Applying Poincar\'e to $f(x)=|x|^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \Var(|X|^2)\le4n\CP(\mu).
  \label{eq:kls-implies-thin-shell}
\end{equation}
There is no known dimension-free converse from this one radial estimate to KLS. It is therefore
accurate to say that thin-shell control alone does not currently prove KLS, but not that such a
converse has been disproved within the log-concave class.

\subsection{The two July 2026 moment-map inputs}

Let $d\nu(y)=e^{-\phi(y)}\dd y$, let $(\nabla\phi)_\#\nu=\mu$, and put
$H=D^2\phi$. For normalized densities $d\mu=e^{-V(x)}\dd x$, the Monge--Amp\`ere equation is
\[
  \log\det H=-\phi+V(\nabla\phi),
\]
and isotropy gives $\E_\nu H=I$. In target coordinates,
$H\circ(\nabla\phi)^{-1}$ is the positive symmetric moment-map Stein kernel
\cite{Fathi2019SteinMomentMaps}.

\begin{theorem}[Chen--Klartag moment-Hessian estimate]
\label{thm:chen-klartag-moment-hessian}
Under the regularity assumptions of \cite{ChenKlartag2026SharpThinShell},
\[
  \E_\nu\norm{H}_{\HS}^2\le2n.
\]
The general log-concave conclusions below follow by the approximation argument in that preprint.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Chen--Klartag sharp thin shell]
\label{thm:chen-klartag-thin-shell}
For every isotropic log-concave $X\in\R^n$,
\[
  \Var(|X|^2)\le8n.
\]
The constant is attained by products of standard centered one-sided exponential variables.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Chen--Klartag sharp third tensor]
\label{thm:chen-klartag-third-moment}
If $X\in\R^n$ is isotropic and log-concave and
$T_3(X)=(\E X_iX_jX_k)_{i,j,k}$, then
\[
  \norm{T_3(X)}_{\HS}^2\le4n.
\]
The preprint also proves sharper convex-body estimates with equality for the regular simplex.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Letwin matrix moment-map estimate]
\label{thm:letwin-moment-map}
Under the regular moment-map assumptions used in \cite{Letwin2026QuadraticKLS}, for every
constant symmetric matrix $B$,
\[
  \E_\nu\Tr(BHBH)\le2\Tr(B^2).
\]
\end{theorem}

After the preprint's approximation argument, Letwin also obtains the arbitrary-law quadratic
Poincar\'e theorem recorded later as Theorem~\ref{thm:letwin-qcts}: for every isotropic
log-concave law and every constant symmetric $M$,
\[
  \Var\langle MX,X\rangle
  \le2\E|\nabla\langle MX,X\rangle|^2
  =8\Tr(M^2).
\]

Theorems~\ref{thm:chen-klartag-moment-hessian}--\ref{thm:letwin-moment-map} are imported from
contemporaneous version-1 preprints \cite{ChenKlartag2026SharpThinShell,Letwin2026QuadraticKLS};
they are not promoted here as independently certified repository proofs. Letwin's $B=I$ case
contains the shared moment-Hessian estimate, while Chen--Klartag's sharp third-tensor, cone,
simplex, and equality analysis is additional. Conversely, Letwin controls every constant
symmetric homogeneous quadratic form. The word \emph{homogeneous} matters: the same sharp
constant is not asserted for arbitrary affine degree-two polynomials.

Only Letwin's preprint supplies the current claimed improvement of the general KLS bound. It
proves $\kappa_n\le2\sqrt2$ for the directional third-moment parameter and combines this with
Klartag's improved Lichnerowicz comparison to obtain
\[
  \sup_\mu\CP(\mu)\lesssim\sqrt{\log n},
  \qquad
  \hstar_n\gtrsim(\log n)^{-1/4}.
\]
The published benchmark remains $\CP\lesssim\log n$, or
$\hstar_n\gtrsim(\log n)^{-1/2}$ \cite{Klartag2023Logarithmic}.

Stochastic localization is the quantitative line behind the successive improvement from
power-law bounds through subpolynomial and polylogarithmic losses to this published
$\sqrt{\log n}$ inverse-Cheeger scale
\cite{LeeVempala2018,Chen2021,KlartagLehec2022Polylog,JambulapatiLeeVempala2022KLS,Klartag2023Logarithmic}.
The two stochastic routes below retain different test-specific information from that machinery:
one follows a candidate cut, while the other follows a first eigenfunction.

\begin{question}[Fixed-eigenfunction absorptive occupation]
\label{q:mm-spectral-occupation}
On smooth strongly log-concave isotropic approximants, let $-Lf=\lambda f$ be a normalized first
nonconstant eigenfunction and, along stochastic localization, set
\[
  g_t=\Cov_{\mu_t}(f,X),\qquad
  H_t=\E_t[(f-\E_tf)(X-a_t)^{\otimes2}],\qquad
  A_t=\Cov_{\mu_t}(X).
\]
Do universal $T_0,C_0,C_1>0$ and $\alpha<1$ exist such that, for every $t\le T_0$,
\[
  \E\int_0^t\norm{H_s}_{\HS}^2\dd s
  \le C_0t+C_1\E\int_0^t|g_s|^2\dd s
  +\alpha\E\int_0^t2g_s^TA_sg_s\dd s,
\]
uniformly through approximation? The fixed-function localization identities show that this
estimate is sufficient for a universal spectral gap; the estimate itself is open.
\end{question}

The detailed manuscript Sections~\ref{sec:introduction}--\ref{sec:open} develop the fixed-cut
Eldan program, with two subroutes. Question~\ref{q:mm-spectral-occupation} records the headline
of the fixed-eigenfunction route, whose full derivation remains in a separate research brief.
Section~\ref{sec:moment-map-cmh} records the deterministic moment-map route at its current audited
level. The control-plane registry, including exact status and retractions, is
\texttt{research/kls/strategy-map.md}.

These routes share a lesson from the July papers: the static constant-matrix problem is no longer
the frontier. Each live route must promote that information while preserving direction and
test-function dependence. Similarity of that loss is not a proof that the route-specific open
statements are equivalent.

\section{The live frontier at a glance}
\label{sec:frontier-atlas}

This section is the shortest complete answer to the questions a reader is most likely to arrive
with: what is being tried, what has actually been established, what blocks each attempt, which
natural variants are already dead, and which attempt currently looks best aligned with what is
known. It is deliberately placed before any route is developed. Everything in it is a pointer;
the statements themselves live in Parts~III and~IV, and the detailed synthesis that revisits them
after the routes have been read is Section~\ref{sec:kls-synthesis}.

\subsection{Suggested external reading order}
\label{subsec:atlas-external-reading}

For a reader coming to the subject rather than to this repository:
\cite{KannanLovaszSimonovits1995} for the conjecture and deterministic localization;
\cite{Eldan2013ThinShell} for stochastic localization and the third-moment parameter;
\cite{LeeVempala2018,LeeVempala2024} for the cleanest entry to the covariance SDE and the
set-transfer argument; \cite{KLnotes} for the best conceptual exposition of localization,
filtering, Bochner, and the operator-norm obstruction; \cite{KlartagLehec2022Polylog} for heat
flow, spectral measures, and $H^{-1}$; \cite{Klartag2023Logarithmic} for improved Lichnerowicz and
the published bound; \cite{Letwin2026QuadraticKLS} for the current preprint record; and
\cite{KlartagLehec2025ThinShell,ChenKlartag2026SharpThinShell} for the strongest related tools and
the clearest illustration of the quadratic-to-all-functions gap.

\subsection{Fences: what is already ruled out}
\label{subsec:atlas-fences}

Negative results are the most reusable part of a search, and four of them constrain everything
above. They are stated where they are proved and collected here so that a reader does not
rediscover a dead variant.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{No uniform operator-norm covariance bound.}
  Proposition~\ref{prop:covariance-spike}: the statement a direct ``bound $\norm{A_t}_\op$
  better'' programme would need is false, for a measure that satisfies KLS.
  \item \textbf{The global weighted package is refuted.}
  Proposition~\ref{prop:weighted-spectator-obstruction} kills the global operator-norm covariance
  weight of subroute E--B; \texttt{q:weighted} is a \emph{refuted} node, not an open one.
  \item \textbf{No matrix-moment argument supplies gate zero.}
  Proposition~\ref{prop:letwin-not-gate-zero}: the fixed-matrix estimate does not close the
  linear sector of Route~C, so Target~2 relocates the average-versus-uniform difficulty rather
  than escaping it.
  \item \textbf{The natural conditional-fiber root frame fails.} Route~F's most obvious
  implementation is refuted outright in Section~\ref{sec:conditional-fiber-frame}, which is why
  the route is stated in terms of an all-frame question instead.
\end{itemize}

Two further cautions are advisory rather than proved, and the ledger records them as
\texttt{heuristic\_barriers} for exactly that reason: the two-tail obstruction
(Obstruction~\ref{obs:two-tail}) and the circularity warning of Section~\ref{sec:excess}. They
guide work; they do not fence a claim.

\subsection{Assessment of priorities}
\label{subsec:atlas-assessment}

Of the four routes, Route~S is the one best aligned with the known obstruction: it is the only one
that avoids asking for a uniform top-covariance statement, and by
Section~\ref{subsec:kls-spike-obstruction} such a statement is false for tensorized exponentials.
That is a structural argument for its priority, not a preference.

Route~C is conceptually deeper and may ultimately be cleaner, since its endpoint is a single
inequality with no stochastic apparatus at all --- but the repository's own experience is that its
difficulty concentrates in a commutator sum that has so far resisted dimension-free control.
Route~E is the most narrowly technical and the most clearly delimited, and it is the one on which
this repository has proved the most, including the negative results. Route~F is the youngest;
its natural implementation is refuted and what remains is a genuine open question rather than a
programme.

One caution belongs beside all four. Three of the four cross-route targets of
Section~\ref{sec:kls-synthesis} take $\kappa_n=O(1)$ as their starting point, and that input is an
unreviewed version-1 preprint. Should it not survive review, the targets do not become wrong, but
their premise reverts to $\CP\lesssim\log n$ and the arithmetic of every ``remaining gap'' claim
changes. The ledger enforces this through \texttt{import\_class: preprint-unreviewed}, which
cannot underlie a node marked \texttt{proved}.
